## Hexagram 1 — ☰ Qián (乾) / Initiating · Creative Power

**Trigrams:** Heaven (☰) over Heaven (☰)
**Keywords:** Creative, initiating, strong, yang, leadership, health

### Judgment
Initiating. Sublime and initiative. Prosperous and smooth. Favorable and beneficial. Steadfast and upright.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Heaven acts with vitality and persistence. In correspondence with this, the superior person keeps himself vital without ceasing.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yang): Dragon lying low. Do not use. — The yang is in the lowest place; time for preparation, not action.
- **Line 2** (yang): Dragon becoming visible in the field. Favorable to see a great person. — His virtue influences extensively.
- **Line 3** (yang): The superior person — all day long, initiating, initiating. At night, keeping alert. Adversity, no fault. — One is on the proper way over and over again.
- **Line 4** (yang): Probably leaping from an abyss. No fault. — In advance there will be no fault; a moment of choice.
- **Line 5** (yang): Dragon flying in the sky. Favorable to see a great person. — There arises a great person to be a leader.
- **Line 6** (yang): Dragon becoming haughty. There is regret. — A state of abundance cannot last long.
- **All Nines**: There appears a group of dragons without a chief. Good fortune. — Following the virtue of Heaven, one should not appear as a chief.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Qian expounds the nature of Nature, the principle of Creation. It is the most sublime, firm, central, and upright gua. Yuan (sublime/initiative), heng (prosperous/smooth), li (favorable/beneficial), zhen (steadfast/upright) are the four attributes of Heaven. The dragon image represents six stages of a superior person's development. The host of the gua is the fifth line: firm, central, correct, the perfect seat of Heaven and of leadership. Confucius says Qian and Kun are the gate of the I Ching. Historically, King Wen derived this gua from his imprisonment by the Shang tyrant, placing it first as the polestar of his revolutionary course.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 1 — Bát Thuần Kiền (八純乾). "Kiền giả, Kiện dã" — Kiền có nghĩa là sức kiện cường, sức sáng tạo của Tạo hóa. Tượng: con Rồng. Quẻ có 6 hào dương — nguyên lực rất mạnh, sáng, hoạt động, không đứng yên. Có 2 nghĩa: (a) về vũ trụ: sức sáng tạo của Trời, Tạo hóa; (b) về việc người: sức sáng tạo của con người. "Nguyên, Hanh, Lợi, Trinh" là 4 đại đức của Kiền. Thánh nhân bảo: "Dịch, hà dã? Trung Chánh nhi dĩ hỉ" — Kinh Dịch là gì? Là Trung Chánh mà thôi. Đại Tượng: "Thiên hành Kiện, Quân tử dĩ tự cường, bất tức" — Hành động của Trời mạnh mẽ; người quân tử noi theo đó, tự cường, không ngừng. Thuộc tháng 4 âm lịch (tháng Tị), Kiền khí lên đến mức cao nhất.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 1: Qián — Creative Power. Heaven's blessing: the creative power awakens a dragon. What had been hidden is now ascending. Take pause before you proceed; be assured of your course of action. Know who you are; be humble and modest, be above reproach. A pillar of beneficent astral light guides your movements. You face a moment of choice: the temporal or the spiritual. Endurance and patience will be required. Sublime success, favors bestowed from Heaven. The qi of Heaven is full of vigor and heals what is ailing. This hexagram in ritual magic invokes the Jade Emperor 玉帝 (Yù Dì); in divination, it is a sign of a blessing from the Heavenly Lord.

## Hexagram 2 — ☷ Kūn (坤) / Responding · Supportive Power

**Trigrams:** Earth (☷) over Earth (☷)
**Keywords:** Receptive, yielding, responsive, earth, nurturing, submission

### Judgment
Responding. Sublimely prosperous and smooth. Favorable with a mare's steadfastness. Superior person has somewhere to go. Predetermining, loses; following, obtains a master. Favorable in the southwest: gets friends. In the northeast: loses friends. Be composed and content. Being steadfast and upright: good fortune.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Earth's nature is submissive. In correspondence with this, the superior person enriches her virtue to sustain all beings.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): Treading on hoarfrost, solid ice will come. — A token of solid ice coming; yin energy is condensing.
- **Line 2** (yin): Straight, square, and great. That's how it is! Nothing is unfavorable. — Spontaneous, operating without effort; the light of Earth carries forward.
- **Line 3** (yin): Containing excellence, appropriate to be steadfast and upright. Probably serving a king; claim no credit, carrying things through to the end.
- **Line 4** (yin): Tie up a bag. No fault, no praise. — Through caution, there will be no harm.
- **Line 5** (yin): A yellow lower garment. Supreme good fortune. — There is beauty within.
- **Line 6** (yin): Dragons playing in the wilderness; their blood is blue-yellow. — Dead end is reached.
- **All Sixes**: Favorable to be perseveringly steadfast and upright. Great will be the end.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Kun represents the Tao of Earth — submission and responding. As human beings one has to be submissive to Heaven and responsive to Heaven's will. The mare, not the stallion, symbolizes Kun's attributes: devoted, submissive, yielding. Southwest (earth direction) finds friends; northeast loses them. The second line (central, correct, yin at yin place) is the host — symbolizing perfect Responding quality. King Wu's overthrow of the Shang tyrant fulfills the All Sixes pattern: all yin lines alternate to yang, completing the Tao of Heaven. Historical context: the gua traces King Wen's family's patient strategy against the Shang, from preparing quietly to the final overthrow.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 2 — Bát Thuần Khôn (八純坤). Khôn là sự thuận tùng, phục tùng. Khôn tượng Đất, phục tùng theo Kiền (Trời). "Tẩn mã chi trinh" — trinh tiết của con ngựa cái. Đất phục tùng Trời, và hết lòng hết dạ phục vụ vạn vật. Khôn là cái Dụng của Đạo Đất, của người làm tôi tớ cho vua. Thuộc tháng 10 âm lịch (tháng Hợi). "Lý sương, kiên băng chí" — thấy sương thì biết băng cứng đã gần, như thấy dấu nhỏ mà tiên đoán việc lớn.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 2: Kūn — Supportive Power. To receive, be receptive. Embrace the power of yielding, and you shall receive. Earth yields to earth: the way to success is by being receptive and yielding. Submit to the Divine and your journey will take you to meet a great master. Be soft, be tender, be accommodating. Leading yourself will lead you astray; follow and you will find your way. Support from the southwest proves to be allies. Hexagram 2 in ritual magic invokes Houtu 后土, the Earth Goddess. The trigrams Earth and Mountain summon the qilin (麒麟), portending a significant, transformative event.

## Hexagram 3 — ䷂ Zhūn (屯) / Beginning · Initial Challenge

**Trigrams:** Water (☵) over Thunder (☳)
**Keywords:** Beginning, difficulty, sprouting, potential, disorder, perseverance

### Judgment
The beginning of a tiny sprout. Sublimely prosperous and smooth. Favorable to be steadfast and upright. Do not act lightly. There is somewhere to go. Favorable to establish feudal lords.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Clouds and thunder fill up. In correspondence with this, the superior person plans and sets things in order.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yang): Lingering and considering. Favorable to abide in being steadfast and upright. Favorable to establish feudal lords. — Upright intention still remains; the superior, respecting the inferior, wins the hearts of all.
- **Line 2** (yin): Difficulty in advancing, hard to proceed. Mounting on horses, still not going forward. Not invading, seeking a marriage. The maiden is chaste, marries not. After ten years, she marries.
- **Line 3** (yin): Chasing deer, no guide in the midst of woods. The superior person is alert: Give up! Going forward: humiliation.
- **Line 4** (yin): Mounting on horses, still not going forward. Seeking a union. Going forward: good fortune. Nothing is unfavorable.
- **Line 5** (yang): Assembling one's abundance. Little things — being steadfast and upright: good fortune. Great things — being steadfast and upright: guard against misfortune.
- **Line 6** (yin): Mounting on horses, still not going forward. Weeping grievously, shedding tears as if bleeding.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Wilhelm translates as "Difficulty at the Beginning"; Huang uses "Beginning." The pictograph of Zhun shows a tiny blade of newly sprouted grass with deep roots — new life in difficulty. Thunder (action) beneath Water (danger): a newly born being with strong roots gathering life force. This gua has all four supreme virtues (yuan, heng, li, zhen), one of only six gua so blessed. King Wen recalled how the Zhou dynasty was like a tiny sprout — favor only by being steadfast and upright. The "maiden" story in multiple lines describes choosing the right alliance. The bottom yang line is the host: even with latent power, the beginning situation is full of potential yet requires extreme care.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 3 — Thủy Lôi Truân (水雷屯). "Truân giả, nan dã" — Truân là khó, là gian khổ ban đầu. Như cây mới mọc khỏi mặt đất vừa phải thoát khỏi sức đè của đất, vừa phải vươn lên. Hình tượng: nước (hiểm) ở trên, lôi (động) ở dưới — sức mạnh đang còn bị kẹt trong hoàn cảnh hiểm trở. Nhưng vì còn có đủ 4 đức Nguyên Hanh Lợi Trinh nên vẫn hanh thông.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 3: Tún — Initial Challenge. Challenges at the beginning leave you feeling defeated; the matter is merely in an undeveloped stage. Failures at the beginning, even repeated missteps, are blessings of strength. When you finally succeed, it will far surpass any success that came before. Initial challenges are the mysterious ways of the Tao. Rain and lightning atop the hill: after the thunderstorm, tensions will be released, bringing order out of confusion. "經綸" (sorting silk threads and braiding them) is a metaphor for planning and governing a rising power. It is a time of personal growth — what you plant now is only a seed, dormant in the earth, but when moisture awakens it, that seed has the power to grow strong enough to split rocks.

## Hexagram 4 — ䷃ Méng (蒙) / Childhood · Naiveté

**Trigrams:** Mountain (☶) over Water (☵)
**Keywords:** Ignorance, education, youth, enlightening, patience, teacher-student

### Judgment
Childhood. Prosperous and smooth. It is not I who seek the ignorant; the ignorant seeks me. On the first divination, I give light. Repeating again is contemptuous. Being contemptuous, I give no more instruction. Favorable to be steadfast and upright.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
A spring flows out of a mountain. The symbol of an unenlightened ignorant. In correspondence with this, the superior person makes every effort to cultivate virtue with resolute deeds.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): Enlightening an ignorant. Favorable to set examples. Operating with shackles, going forward: humiliation.
- **Line 2** (yang): Being magnanimous to an ignorant: good fortune. Taking a maiden as wife: good fortune. The son is able to sustain the family.
- **Line 3** (yin): Do not engage in taking this woman. Catching sight of a handsome man, losing herself. Nothing is favorable.
- **Line 4** (yin): Confining an ignorant: humiliation. — Far from the solid.
- **Line 5** (yin): An ignorant is being enlightened. Good fortune. — She corresponds to humbleness.
- **Line 6** (yang): Disciplining an ignorant. Not favorable to treat like a foe. Favorable to prevent further mischief.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Wilhelm: "Youthful Folly"; Blofeld: "Immaturity"; Huang: "Childhood." The spring from a mountain becomes a murmuring stream — pure and transparent, symbolizing a child's innocent mind. The yang at the second place is the host: firm, magnanimous, able to enlighten others. Education without punishment is the ideal. The ancient Chinese believed every child's wisdom is like uncarved jade, hidden. To educate (qi meng) is to lift the cover. The gua stresses that the ignorant must be humble and seek enlightenment themselves, while the teacher must be patient. King Wen placed this gua to address educating his people in the new dynasty — internal affairs complementing the external (feudal lords) of hexagram 3.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 4 — Sơn Thủy Mông (山水蒙). "Mông giả, mông dã" — Mông là tối tăm, u mê. Núi (cản trở) ở trên, nước (hiểm trở, không chắc chắn) ở dưới. Hình tượng: suối chảy từ chân núi — thuần khiết, trong sáng, tượng trưng tâm trí trong sáng của trẻ thơ. Dạy dỗ người chưa khai sáng là việc thánh thiện.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 4: Méng — Naiveté. You are focused on the wrong inquiry. The Oracle gives no further insight unless the inexperienced fool returns with proper sincerity. Seek better teachings; be aware of your inexperience and acknowledge it. Your modesty will advance you much further than feigning mastery. Hexagram 4 comes up when one is indecisive, changing direction too often, initiating more undertakings than they can finish — stop, settle down, take a breath, focus. The "童" (tóng) carries a dual meaning: innocent child and also shaman (tongji 童乩, spirit medium). "I do not seek the child; the child seeks me" can be read as: "I do not call upon the spirit medium; the spirit medium will call upon me."

## Hexagram 5 — ䷄ Xū (需) / Needing · Patience

**Trigrams:** Water (☵) over Heaven (☰)
**Keywords:** Waiting, patience, nourishment, confidence, timing, trust

### Judgment
Needing. Being sincere and faithful, brilliantly prosperous and smooth. Being steadfast and upright: good fortune. Favorable to cross great rivers.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Clouds ascending in the sky. An image of Needing. In correspondence with this, the superior person eats and drinks, feasting with joy.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yang): One in need waits on the outskirts. Favorable to engage in perseverance. No fault.
- **Line 2** (yang): One in need waits on the sand. A little gossip ends in good fortune.
- **Line 3** (yang): One in need waits in the mud. Result: great trouble to come.
- **Line 4** (yin): One in need waits in the ditch. Get out of the pit. — Following the principle of yielding, accommodate the situation.
- **Line 5** (yang): One in need waits with wine and food. Being steadfast and upright: good fortune. — The position is central and correct.
- **Line 6** (yin): Falling into a pit. There are three unexpected guests coming. Showing respect ends in good fortune.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Wilhelm: "Waiting"; Blofeld: "Calculated Inaction"; Huang: "Needing." The ancient ideograph shows rain descending from clouds, or a priest praying for rain — the primary need in early agricultural society. Water before Heaven: strength confronting danger. The host (fifth line) is central and correct, waiting calmly with wine and food — symbolizing self-confidence and equanimity. King Wen chose Feng ("safe place") to wait with nourishment until the time was right to overthrow the Shang. Three unexpected guests at the top line represent the three former Shang officials who came to King Wen for shelter.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 5 — Thủy Thiên Nhu (水天需). "Nhu giả, tu dã" — Nhu là cần phải chờ đợi, cần phải bổ dưỡng sức mạnh. Khảm (nước, hiểm nguy) ở trên, Kiền (trời, cứng mạnh) ở dưới. Sức mạnh đang phải đối mặt với nguy hiểm — chờ đợi là cần thiết. Có chân thành và kiên định thì mọi sự hanh thông.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 5: Xū — Patience. You seek to advance just when dangers are at their peak. Wait it out. Cultivate patience. Heaven has set the course for an outcome in your favor, but patience is required. Do not force an outcome in a way that would not align with the Divine. Put your faith in the natural flow of events. Clouds form in the sky and rise up toward heaven — the clouds must now wait for the harmony of heaven and earth to discharge its rain. Patience and prayers bring the rain. The sixth line foretells that three spirit omens or uninvited guests will be sent to you; accept help and honor those who help you with sincere gratitude.

## Hexagram 6 — ䷅ Sòng (訟) / Contention · The Trial

**Trigrams:** Heaven (☰) over Water (☵)
**Keywords:** Conflict, dispute, litigation, caution, mediation, mid-course resolution

### Judgment
Contention. Be sincere and truthful; truth is blocked. Be cautious. Midpoint: good fortune. End: misfortune. Favorable to see a great person. Unfavorable to cross great rivers.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Heaven and Water move in opposite directions. There is contention. In correspondence with this, the superior person begins to make plans before he takes action.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): Do not persist in the case. A little dispute ends in good fortune. — Contention should not be long; true or false will be distinguished.
- **Line 2** (yang): Unable to contend. Return and escape. His county of three hundred households: no trouble. — Retreat and hide; in an inferior position contending against a superior one causes your own trouble.
- **Line 3** (yin): Living off ancestors' inheritance. Being steadfast: adversity; ends in good fortune. Probably serving a king, claim no credit.
- **Line 4** (yang): Unable to contend. Turn back; submit to the truth. Changing one's opinion: peace. Being steadfast and upright: good fortune.
- **Line 5** (yang): Dealing with contention. Supreme good fortune. — One is in the central and correct position.
- **Line 6** (yang): Probably a leather belt is given with honor. By the end of the morning — thrice taken away. — To obtain distinction through contention is not worthy of respect.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
The ideograph: speech combined with a picture of opposing and being fair. Heaven (ascending) over Water (descending): conflict inevitable. The host (fifth line) is the arbitrator — firm, strong, central, correct, able to maintain a neutral stand. No satisfying result can ever be obtained at the end of any contention. Compromise and midpoint resolution are key. The gua depicts King Wen's struggle with the Shang tyrant: pleading, being rebuffed, retreating to accumulate strength in patience. The top line: a leather belt given and taken away three times — honor gained through contention cannot last.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 6 — Thiên Thủy Tụng (天水訟). "Tụng giả, tranh dã" — Tụng là kiện tụng, tranh chấp. Kiền (cứng mạnh) ở trên, Khảm (hiểm, quanh co) ở dưới. Bản tánh của hai quẻ vừa cứng vừa quanh co, nên sinh ra tranh cạnh, kiện tụng. Hào thứ 5 là hào chủ — vị phán xử công minh, giải quyết tranh chấp.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 6: Sòng — The Trial. Do not undertake hazardous enterprises. Conditions are ominous. Tensions beyond your control are giving rise to conflicts, but you do possess the power to mitigate harm. Heaven over Water is also an omen of entitlements clashing. The sage will strategize a plan of action before initiating action. The sage prevails in disputes by avoiding them. Hexagram 6 can denote civil suits or tensions between two individuals head to head, in contrast to Hexagram 21 which denotes criminal suits. Seek out the eminent one; until then, it is not auspicious to cross the great stream.

## Hexagram 7 — ䷆ Shī (師) / Multitude · The Army

**Trigrams:** Earth (☷) over Water (☵)
**Keywords:** Army, leadership, discipline, collective purpose, righteous war, mobilization

### Judgment
Multitude. Be steadfast and upright. For a person of noble spirit, good fortune. No fault.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Water contained under Earth. An image of Multitude. In correspondence with this, the superior person embraces people and cares for the multitude.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): Sending out a multitude, it should be kept under control. Otherwise, no good: misfortune.
- **Line 2** (yang): In the midst of the multitude, good fortune. No fault. The king bestows thrice. — Grace from Heaven; thinking of all countries.
- **Line 3** (yin): The multitude perhaps takes charge: misfortune. — They crave greatness and success; no merit.
- **Line 4** (yin): The multitude retreats. No fault. — It does not deviate from the normal course.
- **Line 5** (yin): In the field there are birds, favorable for capture. No fault. The elder should command the multitude. The followers taking charge, being steadfast: misfortune.
- **Line 6** (yin): The great prince issues commands: establish states and hereditary families; inferior persons should not be employed.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Wilhelm and Blofeld: "The Army." Huang: "Multitude." Originally shi meant multitude of people (2,500), not specifically an army. In ancient China, peasants and soldiers were the same group. The second line (only yang, central) is the host. Military action should move forward for a righteous cause only — overthrowing a tyrant or protecting from invasion. King Wen adopted a policy of meeting minority clans with goodwill, educating rather than punishing. The top line: after victory, distribute rewards according to merit; inferior persons should never be employed in authority.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 7 — Địa Thủy Sư (地水師). "Sư giả, chúng dã" — Sư là đông đảo, là binh chúng. Khôn (đất, thuận) ở trên, Khảm (nước, nguy hiểm) ở dưới — tượng đội quân dấu vũ khí trong lòng đất. Chỉ có người chính trực, cao thượng mới đáng lãnh đạo quân đội. Không nên phát động chiến tranh tùy tiện.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 7: Shī — The Army. Your ambitions are too scattered. The ego is a tenacious general who needs to enforce discipline. Your ambitions must serve as a unit, obeying the command of their general. Once you can march with a single, unified sense of purpose, nothing will stop you. Conditions are auspicious when the army marches with a single, unified purpose. The general works toward increasing the kingdom's people and livestock — raise an army to protect their welfare, and for no other purpose. The army and its soldiers are the sum of your skills and capabilities. The Son of Heaven has a destiny to fulfill.

## Hexagram 8 — ䷇ Bǐ (比) / Union · Alliance

**Trigrams:** Water (☵) over Earth (☷)
**Keywords:** Union, alliance, closeness, bond, cooperation, seeking connection

### Judgment
Seeking union. Good fortune. Examine the divination: sublimely persevering, steadfast and upright. No fault. Restless factions just coming. Lagging behind: misfortune.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
On the Earth, there is Water flowing together — an image of Union. In correspondence with this, the ancient king established myriad states and kept a close relationship with the lords.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): With sincerity and truthfulness, seeking union. No fault. Be sincere and truthful, as an earthen vessel that is full. — End coming, there is something. Good fortune.
- **Line 2** (yin): Seeking union from within. Being steadfast and upright: good fortune. — One would not fail.
- **Line 3** (yin): Seeking union with wrong people. — How can it not be hurtful?
- **Line 4** (yin): From without, seeking union. Being steadfast and upright: good fortune. — Follow the one above.
- **Line 5** (yang): An illustration of seeking union: the king drives game on three sides, loses those running out the front. Citizens need no admonition. Good fortune. — Due to its correct central position; discard those who are adverse, accept those who are submissive.
- **Line 6** (yin): Seeking union. Those not having taken the first step: misfortune. — Nothing can be completed.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
The ancient unit of five households was called Bi. Water above Earth: when earth contains water it becomes soft; water on earth flows. The fifth line (only yang, supreme, central and correct) is the host — symbolizing a right person in a right place with great influence. The king's "driving game on three sides" story: King Tang of Shang only spread nets on one side, letting strong animals escape — seeking union by sincerity, not force. Those who come, welcome; those who run off, let go. The top line: one who refuses to seek union at the right time meets misfortune.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 8 — Thủy Địa Tỉ (水地比). "Tỉ giả, phụ dã" — Tỉ là gần gũi, thân mật, hỗ trợ lẫn nhau. Khảm (nước) ở trên, Khôn (đất) ở dưới. Như nước chảy trên mặt đất, đất và nước thân thiết nhau. Muốn được người theo mình, phải thực lòng, không giả tạo.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 8: Bǐ — Alliance. This is the omen of one who follows a good role model, cultivates greatness, then becomes a role model others follow. Alliance of heart and mind. Be a connector, one who brings people together. Kings gain innumerable territories by maintaining close relations with feudal lords. The last chieftain to arrive at the king's court was beheaded (legend of Fangfeng 防風). "Holding a full earthenware vessel" references the Chinese folktale of a prisoner ordered to walk through the streets with a clay pot of water on his head — through motivation and focus, one can achieve anything.

## Hexagram 9 — ䷈ Xiǎo Xū (小畜) / Little Accumulation · Cultivate Gently

**Trigrams:** Wind (☴) over Heaven (☰)
**Keywords:** Small restraint, gentle accumulation, patience, clouds without rain, minor obstacles

### Judgment
Little Accumulation. Prosperous and smooth. Clouds condense, yet no rain at my west side.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Wind blows in the sky. An image of accumulating nourishment by the little. In correspondence with this, the superior person polishes and beautifies his cultured qualities.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)

Heaven between Mountains. An image of Great Accumulation. In correspondence with this, the superior person has a wide learning and memory of the words and deeds of the past, in order to accumulate his virtue.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Da means great; Xü means accumulation. Wilhelm: "The Taming Power of the Great"; Blofeld: "The Great Nourisher." Mountain (keeping still) over Heaven (creative power): the creative power is kept still — accumulation. The spirit: those who have accumulated wealth and wisdom know when to release. "Eat not at home" — go out and search for virtuous people to serve the country. Fourth line: board on young bull's horns (prevent hurting before it happens) = supreme good fortune. Fifth line: castrating the boar (dealing with the fundamental cause) = congratulation. Top line: at last, Heaven's thoroughfare is open — the way grandly opens for advance.

Hexagram 26: Dà Chù — Cultivate the Supreme. You hold great power and potential, but restraint is necessary to temper that power. Study the words and deeds of the great and the wise before you; that way you can build and develop your virtue. Do not eat meals from home — do not take from your own people. It is auspicious to cross the great stream. The fourth and fifth lines are both about taming the wild instinct, the fearsome power of nature.

## Hexagram 27 — ䷚ Yí (頤) / Nourishing · Receive Nourishment

**Trigrams:** Mountain (☶) over Thunder (☳)
**Keywords:** Nourishment, jaws, what we eat and speak, self-cultivation, nurturing, wisdom of nourishing

### Judgment
Nourishing. Being steadfast and upright: good fortune. Watch your nourishment; pay attention to what is in your mouth.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Thunder beneath Mountain. An image of Nourishing. In correspondence with this, the superior person is careful of his words and moderate in eating and drinking.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yang): Putting aside your spiritual tortoise, staring at me with mouth drooling. Misfortune. — Staring with drooling mouth; it is not a noble manner.
- **Line 2** (yin): Turning upside down, seeking nourishment. Contrary to normal. From the hill, seeking nourishment. Moving forward: misfortune.
- **Line 3** (yin): Contrary to normal, seeking nourishment. Being steadfast: misfortune. Ten years, not used. Nothing is favorable.
- **Line 4** (yin): Turning upside down, seeking nourishment. Good fortune. A tiger's glare, covetous, covetous. Its desire, chasing, chasing. No fault. — Good fortune in turning upside down; the one above sheds light.
- **Line 5** (yin): Contrary to normal. Abide in being steadfast and upright. Good fortune. Not appropriate to cross great rivers. — Due to devotedly following the one above.
- **Line 6** (yang): From this nourishing, adversity: good fortune. Favorable to cross great rivers. — There is great blessing.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Yi originally meant the chin, the lower jaw — extended to include nourishing. Upper and lower yang lines: the jaws. Four broken yin lines: the teeth. The structure looks like a mouth. Mountain (still upper jaw) above Thunder (moving lower jaw): the motion of chewing. Ancient Chinese nourishing included nurturing virtue — nourishing without nurturing was the way of animals (Mencius). The top line (host) is the source of nourishment for all. "Heaven and earth nourish all beings. The holy sages nourish the virtuous, and thus reach all." King Wu provided physical and spiritual nourishment to the conquered Shang subjects.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 27: Yí — Receive Nourishment. Be open to receiving; receive to nourish. The true measure of who you are is in how you nourish your body and what words you let out of your mouth. Right now is the time to feed yourself to provide yourself with sustenance. The six lines portray different ways people seek nourishment — from self-reliant nourishment (best) to parasitic or exploitative seeking (worst). The sixth line: the auspices of a destined hero; the uppermost line yang is whom all below look up to and rely upon for support.

## Hexagram 28 — ䷛ Dà Guò (大過) / Great Exceeding · Undertake the Great

**Trigrams:** Lake (☱) over Wind (☴)
**Keywords:** Great excess, ridgepole bending, extraordinary action, crisis, standing alone, imbalance

### Judgment
Great Exceeding. The ridgepole sags. Favorable to have somewhere to go. Prosperous and smooth.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
The lake rises over the tree. An image of Great Exceeding. In correspondence with this, the superior person stands alone without fear and withdraws from the world with no depression.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): For a mat, use white cogon grasses. No fault. — It is to put soft materials underneath.
- **Line 2** (yang): Withered willow produces sprouts; old man takes a young wife. Nothing is unfavorable. — The excess and the insufficiency can be adjusted.
- **Line 3** (yang): Ridgepole sags. Misfortune. — Being unable to get support.
- **Line 4** (yang): Ridgepole curves upward. Good fortune. If responding with others, humiliation. — Rely not on support from below.
- **Line 5** (yang): Withered willow produces flowers. Old woman takes a young husband. No fault. No praise. — How could this last long?
- **Line 6** (yin): Passing through a river, water over head. Misfortune. No fault. — There is no ground for blame.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Da: great; Guo: exceeding. Wilhelm: "Preponderance of the Great." The structure shows a ridgepole (the topmost horizontal beam): the four strong yang lines in the middle make it heavy, while the two weak yin lines at either end cannot support the weight. The fourth line (host) is firm and balanced — able to carry a heavy burden and curve upward. The principle of adjustment between strong/weak, excessive/deficient: "moderate the yang with the yin and elevate the yin with the yang." Like floating with a favorable wind makes people happy to follow. Extraordinary situation requires extraordinary action.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 28 — Trạch Phong Đại Quá (澤風大過). "Đại Quá giả, tài đại giả, quá dã" — Đại Quá là vượt quá mức thái quá. Đoài (đầm) ở trên, Tốn (gió, gỗ) ở dưới — đầm tràn ngập gỗ. ### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 28: Dà Guò — Undertake the Great. The many steps you've taken and the changes at every step have converged. You are at a critical juncture on the cusp of undertaking the great. Wind that lifts the Lake begets excessive force. A fundamental change is about to take place, and you are at its center. The sage is independent and fearless. A fragile, unstable roof beam — a critical situation. Renouncing the world you knew. Expelling melancholy. It is a tipping point. The fourth line references a snake reminiscent of the coiled snake Shakti (kundalini) dwelling at the base of the spine — this hexagram expresses kundalini awakening.

## Hexagram 31 — ䷞ Xián (咸) / Mutual Influence · Mutual Accord

**Trigrams:** Lake (☱) over Mountain (☶)
**Keywords:** Courtship, mutual influence, attraction, sensation, wooing, open heart, beginning of Lower Canon

### Judgment
Mutual Influence. Prosperous and smooth. Favorable to be steadfast and upright. Take a maiden as a wife. Good fortune.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
Lake on Mountain. An image of Mutual Influence. In correspondence with this, the superior person opens his heart and mind to accept people without prejudice.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): Mutual influence on one's big toes. — His will is directed outward.
- **Line 2** (yin): Mutual influence on one's calves. Misfortune. Tarrying: good fortune. — Be yielding and reasonable; there will be no harm.
- **Line 3** (yang): Mutual influence on one's thighs. Insist on his following. Going forward: humiliation. — Hard to get along with one another; insisting on following others, one holds a low standard.
- **Line 4** (yang): Being steadfast and upright: good fortune. Regret vanishes. Hesitating, hesitating, coming or going? Friends follow your thought. — One has not yet become brilliant and great.
- **Line 5** (yang): Mutual influence on one's upper back. No regret. — His will is skin deep.
- **Line 6** (yin): Mutual influence on one's jaw, cheek, and tongue. — The open mouth makes empty promises.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Xian originally meant "all, together, mutual." Hui-yuan: "The root of the I Ching is mutual influence." Lake (youngest daughter, joyous, sinking) over Mountain (youngest son, keeping still, towering up) — they mutually influence each other like a man and woman courting. This gua begins the Lower Canon, paralleling Qian and Kun in the Upper Canon. The fourth line (at the heart position) is the host — mutual influence should radiate from the heart, free from selfish motives. The Duke of Zhou uses body parts (toes to jaw) to expound six stages of seeking mutual influence. The sixth line: fond of sweet words and honeyed phrases — if one merely makes empty promises, there is no true mutual influence.

Quẻ 31 — Trạch Sơn Hàm (澤山咸). "Hàm giả, cảm dã" — Hàm (Cảm) là cảm ứng, cảm động. Đoài (đầm) ở trên, Cấn (núi) ở dưới — đầm ngậm nước ở trên núi, núi đỡ đầm — 2 thứ hỗ trợ, cảm ứng nhau. Hexagram 31: Xián — Mutual Accord. Now is the time to cultivate influence. Attract what you seek without pursuing. Receive others with humility and nonjudgment. On their own accord they'll come. Endeavor to give more than you receive, and the paradox of life is that this will grant you more than you give. The character 咸 (Xián) with the addition of 心 (heart) becomes 感 (gǎn) — emotion and sentimentality. Xián is about sensation, feelings, and influencing another through touch. This is one of the four Love and Romance Hexagrams (31, 32, 53, 54). Hexagram 31 refers to the mutual attraction and sexual chemistry experienced by the young couple in love.

## Hexagram 32 — ䷟ Héng (恆) / Long Lasting · The Eternal

**Trigrams:** Thunder (☳) over Wind (☴)
**Keywords:** Perseverance, duration, endurance, long-lasting relationship, constancy, thunder and wind together

### Judgment
Long Lasting. Prosperous and smooth. No fault. Favorable to be steadfast and upright. Favorable to have somewhere to go.
<!-- src: huang -->

### The Image
The union of Thunder and Wind. An image of Long Lasting. In correspondence with this, the superior person stands firm without changing his aim.
<!-- src: huang -->

### Lines (Moving Lines)
- **Line 1** (yin): Deeply long lasting. Being steadfast: misfortune. Nothing is favorable. — Misfortune of deeply long lasting; seeking deepness at the very beginning.
- **Line 2** (yang): Regret vanishes. — It holds to the central place.
- **Line 3** (yang): Not long lasting, one's virtue. Probably meets with disgrace. Being steadfast: humiliation. — No one tolerates him.
- **Line 4** (yang): Field. No birds. — Staying away from one's proper place for long, how can one catch game?
- **Line 5** (yin): Long lasting, one's virtue. Being steadfast and upright. Wives: good fortune. Husbands: misfortune. — Docility brings good fortune for a wife; a man must make resolute decisions.
- **Line 6** (yin): Long lasting agitation. Misfortune. — Long-lasting agitation at the uppermost position; greatly without merit.

### Alfred Huang's Commentary <!-- src: huang -->
Heng means persistence, perseverance, long lasting. Wilhelm: "Duration"; Blofeld: "The Long Enduring." This gua is the inverse of Xian (Mutual Influence) — if Xian is the newlyweds, Heng represents the long-term union of the old married couple. Thunder (eldest son, outer/yang) above Wind (eldest daughter, inner/yin): "firm is above, gentle below; Thunder and Wind are in mutual support." The ancient Chinese marriage concept: husband takes the initiative, wife is subordinate. The I Ching's central wisdom: "Sun and moon rely on the Tao of Heaven; thus can their shining be long lasting. The four seasons change and transform; thus can their production of beings long endure. The holy sage remains long lasting in his way of life; then all things under Heaven are transformed to completion." The second yang line (host) represents the one who walks in the central path — the only way to have enduring effects.

### Vietnamese Commentary [vi] <!-- src: viet -->
Quẻ 32 và 31 thuộc quyển Hạ nên quyển Thượng (01_viet_iching_vol1.md) không có phần bình chú cho 2 quẻ này.

### Wen Benebell's Commentary <!-- src: wen -->
Hexagram 32: Héng — The Eternal. You are being tasked to handle more than you should be responsible for. The action to take is to endure, and the way to endure is to change. Within you, you must pursue a constant state of transformation and change, though your outward demeanor should be one of conviction, firmness, and resolve. Be mindful — as you change, you may be tempted to call others to change; you cannot force another's transformation. Seek alliance with those most different from you, and find harmony. Hexagram 32 is the midpoint of the I Ching (32 of 64 in both King Wen's order and the Mawangdui order). The doctrine of the Eternal (恒道 Héng Dào) as the Tao expresses enduring change; to achieve immortality is to never cease cultivation 工夫 (gōng fū). Hexagram 32 is one of the four Love and Romance Hexagrams (31, 32, 53, 54) — here referring to the institution of marriage continuing the narrative from Hexagram 31.
