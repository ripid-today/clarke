## What Are Trigrams?

The eight trigrams (八卦, Bā Guà) are the eight classical building blocks of Taoist metaphysics. Each trigram is a three-line symbol composed of unbroken (yang, ⚊) and broken (yin, ⚋) lines. They represent the eight triplicities from which all matter is constructed — comparable to Platonic classical elements but more granular.

When we say Gua (卦), we can be referring to either the eight trigrams or the sixty-four hexagrams. The eight Gua, or trigrams, are the most essential units of substance, whereas the sixty-four Gua, or hexagrams, are the most essential units of change. Each hexagram is composed of a pair of trigrams.

**Origin:** Fu Xi (伏羲) is credited with devising the eight primary trigrams around 2600 BC. These early trigrams formed an archaic writing system for communicating with spirits. King Wen later combined the eight trigrams in paired combinations to form the sixty-four hexagrams.

**Lines:** A yang line (unbroken, ——) is firm, strong, active, masculine. A yin line (broken, — —) is yielding, receptive, passive, feminine.

### Notes on Family Assignments
- Heaven (Qián) and Earth (Kūn) = Father and Mother (pure yang, pure yin)
- Zhen (Thunder), Kan (Water), Gen (Mountain) = Eldest, Middle, Youngest **Sons** (yang trigrams: one yang line among two yin)
- Xun (Wind), Li (Fire), Dui (Lake) = Eldest, Middle, Youngest **Daughters** (yin trigrams: one yin line among two yang)

## Extended Correspondences by Trigram

### ☰ Heaven (Qián 乾)
- **Symbol:** Three unbroken yang lines
- **Nature (Qi):** Naturally occurring creative force that asserts itself; innovation; ingenuity; divine inspiration; petitioning gods; attunement to the celestial realm
- **Key Associations (Ten Wings):** The circle; jade; metal; the fruit of trees; the sublime red color
- **Moon Phase:** Full moon
- **Planet:** Venus (金星, Metal Star)
- **Zodiac Animals:** Dog, Boar
- **Materials:** Gold, jade, precious stones, mirrors
- **Weather:** Clear and bright skies; ice and cold
- **Personages:** Elders, seniors, leaders, celebrities, aristocracy, positions of authority
- **Land Forms:** Verdant meadows, gardens, lush landscapes, the capital city
- **Building Structures:** Public or government buildings
- **Traits:** Tall, regal, shows integrity, ethereal, decisive, exudes power and prestige, clarity of mind
- **Sound:** Steadily paced rhythms, a slow drumbeat
- **Wilhelm name:** The Creative

### ☷ Earth (Kūn 坤)
- **Symbol:** Three broken yin lines
- **Nature (Qi):** Naturally occurring receptive force; receiving; absorbing and then nurturing or cultivating; the fertile field for growth; loving romantic relationships; connects to the underworld
- **Key Associations:** The cauldron, a wagon, black soil, woven textiles, variety and multitudes
- **Moon Phase:** New moon
- **Planet:** Saturn (土星, Earth Star)
- **Zodiac Animals:** Sheep, Monkey
- **Materials:** Clay, silk, grains, cotton, fabrics
- **Weather:** Misty, light rain, cloudy, dank or dark
- **Personages:** Queen, mother, farmer, artisan, the masses; manufacturing, fashion, retail, marketing
- **Land Forms:** Open fields, rural, country, small town
- **Building Structures:** Basement, smaller houses, earth-toned buildings
- **Traits:** Open and receptive, devoted, faithful, loyal, nurturing, easygoing, passive, patient, calm; larger belly or thicker torso; square features
- **Sound:** Silence; or night sounds, crickets chirping
- **Wilhelm name:** The Receptive

### ☳ Thunder (Zhèn 震)
- **Symbol:** Single yang line below two yin lines (yang at bottom)
- **Nature (Qi):** Arouse; shake; jolt; incite; divisive force with intent to unite; awaken a sense of purpose; to stir up; stimulate; defensive magic; electrify; galvanize; hand of God
- **Key Associations:** Roadways, road openers, bamboo, vengeance and vehemence
- **Moon Phase:** Waxing crescent
- **Planet:** Jupiter (木星)
- **Zodiac Animal:** Rabbit
- **Materials:** Wood, wooden instruments, drums
- **Weather:** Thunder and lightning
- **Personages:** Age thirty to forty; fast-paced business personnel, corporate, entertainers; artisans; R&D
- **Land Forms:** Forests, dense woods, parched but grassy areas
- **Building Structures:** Highways and busy streets; skyscrapers, tall buildings
- **Traits:** Proud, good-looking, successful, anxious, musical, quick-tempered, talented
- **Sound:** Clamorous, boisterous, cries of protest, rowdy
- **Wilhelm name:** The Arousing

### ☴ Wind (Xùn 巽)
- **Symbol:** Single yin line below two yang lines (yin at bottom)
- **Nature (Qi):** Gentle influence; a soft force; diffusion; genial; humane; agreeable; pleasing; pliable; cultivated; adaptable; flexible; clearing to increase qi flow; mental acuteness; discernment; to permeate and diffuse; The Ten Wings describes Wind as the Trigram of Decision
- **Key Associations:** (per Ten Wings) The trigram of decision; working wood; long, diffuse
- **Moon Phase:** Waning gibbous
- **Planet:** Jupiter (木星)
- **Zodiac Animals:** Dragon, Snake
- **Materials:** Wood and flexible materials
- **Weather:** Breezy, windy, variable
- **Personages:** Persons with gentle manner, scholars, travelers
- **Traits:** Skillful, diplomatic, flexible, inclined toward intellectual or spiritual pursuits
- **Sound:** Soft wind, hushed
- **Wilhelm name:** The Gentle (The Penetrating, Wind)

### ☵ Water (Kǎn 坎)
- **Symbol:** Single yang line between two yin lines (yang in center)
- **Nature (Qi):** The moon; lunar forces; abyss; the darkest of depths; danger; risk; rushing rivers; the arcane and the occult; career success; flowing, fluent, shifting, indefinite force; the shapeshifter; The Ten Wings describes Water as the trigram of the blood
- **Key Associations:** Channels and ditches (that which is hidden and concealed); the wheel; thieves
- **Moon Phase:** Third quarter
- **Planet:** Mercury (水星, Water Star)
- **Zodiac Animal:** Rat
- **Materials:** Medicines, healing soups, dark mirrors
- **Weather:** Heavy rains, snow, gloomy, dew, frost
- **Personages:** Persons in their twenties or thirties; medical professionals, nurses, health care, diplomats, international relations; underhanded statecraft; cunning
- **Land Forms:** Bodies of water, coastal terrain, rivers, the sea
- **Building Structures:** Hospitals, restaurants and bars, cafes, fisherman's wharf
- **Traits:** Emotional, intense, headstrong, pensive, melancholic, prone to depression, inclination toward esoteric knowledge, soft features
- **Sound:** Mantra recitations, prayers, minor keys, lamentations, nocturnes
- **Wilhelm name:** The Abysmal (Water)

### ☲ Fire (Lí 離)
- **Symbol:** Single yin line between two yang lines (yin in center)
- **Nature (Qi):** The Sun; solar forces; also associated with lightning (per Ten Wings); radiate; attract; cling; enliven; inspire; illuminate; clarity; rapid movement; logical reasoning; promotion; public recognition; applied sciences; technical know-how
- **Key Associations:** The helmet, the sword, the spear
- **Moon Phase:** First quarter
- **Planet:** Mars (火星, Fire Star)
- **Zodiac Animal:** Horse
- **Materials:** That which heats or gives light
- **Weather:** Sunny and clear; risk of drought
- **Personages:** Intellectuals, students, soldiers, a wise person, leader, manager, sales
- **Land Forms:** Deserts, arid plains; can also indicate lushly fertile land black with ash
- **Building Structures:** Kitchen, library, cultural centers
- **Traits:** Ambitious, active, assertive, extroverted, quick to agitate, intelligent, quick learner
- **Sound:** Fast tempo and higher-pitched
- **Wilhelm name:** The Clinging (Fire)

### ☶ Mountain (Gěn 艮)
- **Symbol:** Single yang line above two yin lines (yang at top)
- **Nature (Qi):** Stillness; to stop; to halt; serenity; tranquility; inactivity; quietude; lull; standstill; preserving; stabilizing; balancing force; cultivating knowledge; excelling in ceremonial magic (方術)
- **Key Associations:** Gateways, vines, tall and strong trees
- **Moon Phase:** Waning crescent
- **Planet:** Saturn (土星, Earth Star)
- **Zodiac Animals:** Ox, Tiger
- **Materials:** Stone, earth
- **Weather:** Clear and still
- **Personages:** Peak of life; self-assured and independent individuals; those who are resistant to change
- **Land Forms:** Mountains, rocky terrain, solitary peaks
- **Traits:** Skillful, clever, introverted, stubborn, stagnant, quiet unless spoken to, resistant to change, pensive, philosophical
- **Sound:** Deep, resonant, stable
- **Wilhelm name:** Keeping Still (Mountain)

### ☱ Lake (Duì 兌)
- **Symbol:** Single yin line above two yang lines (yin at top)
- **Nature (Qi):** Nourishing, sustaining force; creating a servitor; business success; law; exchange of energy — one reflects the other in equal and opposite manner; mutual joy; winning legal matters and/or negotiations; the sorceress (per Ten Wings: 兌為澤，為少女，為巫 — Lake governs the marshes, the youngest daughter, the wu shaman)
- **Key Associations:** The sorceress; harvest; salt
- **Moon Phase:** Waxing gibbous
- **Planet:** Venus (金星, Metal Star)
- **Zodiac Animal:** Rooster
- **Materials:** Silver, light metals, ornamentals, metal instruments
- **Weather:** Rainy and cloudy
- **Personages:** Young children, hospitality service occupations, writers, researchers; law
- **Land Forms:** Riverbanks, marshes, wetlands, valleys
- **Building Structures:** Walls, metal-heavy urban areas, fences
- **Traits:** Happy, charismatic, expressive, a good writer or orator, likes to gossip, does not take criticism well
- **Sound:** Chimes, sound of bells, clanging
- **Wilhelm name:** The Joyous (Lake)

## Yin and Yang Composition of the Eight Trigrams

The trigrams are generated from yin (—) and yang (——) through a hierarchical expansion:

1. **Wuji** (無極) — Numinous Nothingness
2. **Taiji** (太極) — Numinous Omnipresence
3. **Pair of Affinities** — Yin (陰) and Yang (陽)
4. **Four Faces of God** (四象):
   - 太陰 Elder yin
   - 少陽 Younger yang
   - 少陰 Younger yin
   - 太陽 Elder yang
5. **Eight Trigrams** (八卦) — Each of the Four Faces adds a yin or yang line to produce the eight trigrams

**Fuxi's Sequence (table 5-5 in Wen source):**

| # | Trigram | Chinese | Lines (bottom→top) |
|---|---------|---------|---------------------|
| 1 | Heaven | 乾 Qián | ——, ——, —— (all yang) |
| 2 | Lake | 兌 Duì | ——, ——, — — |
| 3 | Fire | 離 Lí | ——, — —, —— |
| 4 | Thunder | 震 Zhèn | ——, — —, — — |
| 5 | Wind | 巽 Xùn | — —, ——, —— |
| 6 | Water | 坎 Kǎn | — —, ——, — — |
| 7 | Mountain | 艮 Gěn | — —, — —, —— |
| 8 | Earth | 坤 Kūn | — —, — —, — — (all yin) |

The bottom four trigrams (1–4) have yang as the bottommost line; the top four (5–8) have yin as the bottommost line. This mirrors the Taijitu yin-yang symbol.

**Yang trigrams** (one yang line among two yin): Zhen (Thunder), Kan (Water), Gen (Mountain) → represent the three sons.
**Yin trigrams** (one yin line among two yang): Xun (Wind), Li (Fire), Dui (Lake) → represent the three daughters.

## Prior Heaven Arrangement — Fuxi's Ba Gua (Xian Tian 先天)

The Early Heaven sequence (先天八卦) was mythologized as the arrangement revealed to Fuxi. It corresponds with the Hé Tú (河圖) River Map and reveals the natural cosmic cycles of creation and destruction.

**Directional layout:**
- South: Heaven (Qián) ☰
- North: Earth (Kūn) ☷
- East: Li (Fire) ☲
- West: Kan (Water) ☵
- Southeast: Lake (Duì) ☱
- Northwest: Thunder (Zhèn) ☳ *(in some reconstructions)*
- Northeast: Mountain (Gěn) ☶
- Southwest: Wind (Xùn) ☴

According to the Hé Tú pattern: The changing younger yang and younger yin flow together along the south axis and the east. The matured elder yang and elder yin flow together along the north and the west axis.

**Key principle:** Fuxi's Ba Gua represents the natural cosmic order of creation. Opposing trigrams are polar opposites in terms of their line composition (Heaven is all yang; Earth is all yin; Fire and Water are each other's inversions; etc.).

## Later Heaven Arrangement — King Wen's Ba Gua (Hou Tian 後天)

The Later Heaven sequence (後天八卦) was the rearrangement credited to King Wen. It corresponds with the Luò Shū (洛書) Lo Shu magic square and reveals the secrets of controlling natural cosmic cycles — the applied sciences for divination, feng shui, and ceremonial magic.

**Directional layout (King Wen's arrangement):**
- South: Fire (Lí) ☲ — Summer Solstice
- North: Water (Kǎn) ☵ — Winter Solstice
- East: Thunder (Zhèn) ☳ — Spring Equinox, sun rising
- West: Lake (Duì) ☱ — Autumn Equinox, sun setting
- Northeast: Mountain (Gěn) ☶
- Southwest: Earth (Kūn) ☷
- Southeast: Wind (Xùn) ☴
- Northwest: Heaven (Qián) ☰

*Note: In Alfred Huang's source, the four cardinal directions under King Wen's arrangement are: East = Thunder, West = Lake, South = Fire, North = Water; Southwest = Earth, Northeast = Mountain, Southeast = Wind, Northwest = Heaven.*

**Two governing rules of King Wen's arrangement:**
1. **Rule of Symmetry (1:1 trigrams):** If a trigram is symmetrical, the yin line in one trigram changes to yang in its polarity (trigram at direct 180° opposite position). Example: Fire ⇆ Water; Heaven ⇆ Earth.
2. **Rule of Inversion (2+1 trigrams):** When there are two lines of the same plus one different, the trigram first flips on itself, then all lines change polarity, to transform into the opposite position. Example: Wind ⇆ Mountain; Thunder ⇆ Lake.

**Lo Shu numerology in King Wen's Ba Gua:**
- 1 = Water (North)
- 2 = Earth (Southwest)
- 3 = Thunder (East)
- 4 = Wind (Southeast)
- 5 = Center
- 6 = Heaven (Northwest)
- 7 = Lake (West)
- 8 = Mountain (Northeast)
- 9 = Fire (South)

**Key principle:** Fuxi's Early Heaven reveals what is (natural order); King Wen's Later Heaven reveals how to work with it (applied knowledge, divination, feng shui, ritual magic).

## How Hexagrams Are Formed

Each hexagram is formed by combining two trigrams — an upper trigram (outer gua, 外卦) and a lower trigram (inner gua, 內卦):

- The **lower three lines** (lines 1–3, counting from the bottom) = **lower trigram** = inner gua
- The **upper three lines** (lines 4–6) = **upper trigram** = outer gua
- The lower gua represents the inner situation; the upper gua represents the outer situation

When reading a hexagram, the commentaries will describe it as "Upper Gua over Lower Gua" (e.g., "Kun above, Qian below" = Earth over Heaven = Hexagram 12).

Eight trigrams × eight trigrams = 64 possible hexagram combinations.

Furthermore, each trigram combines with every other trigram (including itself) in fifteen combinations total — the number fifteen representing the harmony and order of the universe in Taoist numerology (also the sum of any row, column, or diagonal in the Lo Shu magic square).

## Nuclear Trigrams (Mutual Gua 互卦)

**What they are:** The nuclear trigrams (also called mutual trigrams or inner trigrams) are the two hidden trigrams found within any hexagram. They reveal the hidden, inner meaning or dynamic underlying the hexagram.

**How to identify them:**
- Take lines 2, 3, and 4 of the hexagram → these form the **lower nuclear trigram** (inner mutual gua)
- Take lines 3, 4, and 5 of the hexagram → these form the **upper nuclear trigram** (outer mutual gua)
- Stack them: lower nuclear beneath upper nuclear → this produces the **mutual hexagram** (互卦, Hù Guà)

**Significance:** The ancient sages considered lines 2–5 to be the heart of any six-line gua. The hidden meaning of any hexagram lies in its mutual gua; it should not be ignored. Reading the mutual hexagram's name, symbol, King Wen's Decision, and Confucius's Commentary reveals deeper, latent content.

*(Source: Alfred Huang, The Complete I Ching, lines 629, 14515)*

## Trigram Pair Relationships and Dynamics

When two trigrams are combined into a hexagram, their interaction and relationship carry meaning:

- **Heaven + Earth (Qian + Kun):** The fundamental pair — pure yang and pure yin, creative force and responsive force. Heaven initiates; Earth responds, receives, and completes.
- **Fire + Water (Li + Kan):** In opposition and complementary. Within yang there is yin (Fire: yin center); within yin there is yang (Water: yang center). Combined they form the last two hexagrams of the received order (63 and 64).
- **Thunder + Wind (Zhen + Xun):** Give momentum. Thunder is the hand of God; Wind diffuses. Thunder over Wind (Hexagram 32) = endurance; Wind over Thunder (Hexagram 42) = increase and gain.
- **Lake + Mountain (Dui + Gen):** Matron and patron forces of all nature spirits. Lake over Mountain (Hexagram 31) = attraction and amplification; Mountain over Lake (Hexagram 41) = repulsion and reduction.

**Line position dynamics within a hexagram:**
- Yang lines are "correct" at positions 1 (initial), 3, and 5 (odd positions)
- Yin lines are "correct" at positions 2, 4, and 6 (top) (even positions)
- The second and fifth places are **central** (middle of each trigram) — generally most auspicious
- Lines **correspond** when they form a yin-yang pair at: positions 1 & 4, 2 & 5, 3 & 6
- A line **carries** the line above it (yin carrying yang = auspicious, as a horse carries a rider)
- A line **mounts** the line below it (yang mounting yin = auspicious, as a rider mounts a horse)

## Trigram Line Count and Host Lines

In each primary trigram, the **host** is the principal line:
- Heaven (☰): host at the central place (line 2 of the trigram)
- Earth (☷): host at the central place
- Water (☵): host at the central place
- Fire (☲): host at the central place
- Wind (☴): host at the bottom (line 1 of the trigram — the single yin line)
- Thunder (☳): host at the bottom (line 1 — the single yang line)
- Lake (☱): host at the top (line 3 — the single yin line)
- Mountain (☶): host at the top (line 3 — the single yang line)

*(Source: Alfred Huang, The Complete I Ching, glossary entry "Host," lines 14465–14473)*

## Hexagram–Trigram Lookup

To find a hexagram number: locate the upper trigram column and lower trigram row in the intersection table at the back of any standard I Ching. The eight trigrams produce 64 unique combinations (including each trigram doubled with itself).

The eight "doubled" hexagrams (same trigram above and below) are: Hexagrams 1 (Heaven/Heaven), 2 (Earth/Earth), 29 (Water/Water), 30 (Fire/Fire), 51 (Thunder/Thunder), 52 (Mountain/Mountain), 57 (Wind/Wind), 58 (Lake/Lake).
