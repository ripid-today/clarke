## Overview

The original casting method for the I Ching was with yarrow stalks, though the underlying means for divining is numerology. Thus, the method can vary so long as the mathematical basis does not.

**Historical timeline of methods:**
- Yarrow stalk method: canonical since ~221 BC (Ten Wings, Xici 繫辭傳); recreated by Zhu Xi in AD 1188
- Coin method: records surface around AD 200 (Three Kingdoms era); popularized by Tang dynasty (~AD 618)
- Simple yarrow variant: popularized in Japan by Nemoto Michiaki (1897), using 45 stalks rather than 49
- Plum Blossom numerology (梅花易數): devised by Shao Yong (邵雍, 1011–1077); mathematics-based, no physical casting required

**Underlying cosmological logic of all methods:** Each step of the casting process replicates Taoist cosmological principles — the One becomes Two (yin/yang), the Two become Four (Four Faces of God), the Four become Eight (the Ba Gua), and the Eight combine into Sixty-Four (the hexagrams).

## Preparations Before the Divination

*(Source: Wen, ch. 7)*

**The space:**
- Ideally dedicate a room or area to divination and spiritual cultivation; place the table along the north side with an entrance facing south
- Approach the divination table from the east; leave toward the west (in acknowledgement of the sun's path)
- Wash the table and tidy the space; burn three sticks of incense to cleanse the area
- Set out all tools: paper, writing utensils, books, incense holder, lighter, a divination mat, optional candles

**Timing:**
- Prepare the space; then return a few hours later or the next day — whatever feels right
- Wash your hands before commencing
- Sit in stillness for a moment; contemplate the matter; light incense

**Attire and posture:**
- Dress respectfully and neatly; assume someone is watching
- Pay attention to posture; maintain a calm, humble, sincere state throughout

**Yarrow stalk care (when not in use):**
- Wrap stalks in scented silk; keep inside a pouch made from black or very dark cloth (black = symbolic of wu wei and the One from which myriad things arose)
- Keep the pouch in a cylindrical bamboo or wooden tube; place the tube along the north side of your divination table or altar
- Burn a stick of incense daily at the divination table, even when not divining — this maintains the forceful qi of the tool

*(Alfred Huang also recommends:)* Wrap the book and stalks separately in silk or cloth. Place both on a shelf at about eye level. Always wash hands before divination. The diviner should ideally face north (listening to the divine spirit, which is in the center, facing south).

## Question Formulation

*(Sources: Wen ch. 7; Alfred Huang)*

- In every divination, ask only **one question**
- The question should be **simple and clear**
- Avoid vague or optional questions; avoid asking about multiple subjects
- For beginners: the best inquiry is for **advice**, not prediction
- Do not consult the I Ching when you already know what you should do according to common sense and moral principles — divine only for important questions
- Never consult for mean purposes or with selfish motivations
- Any question can be presented, but how you approach interpretation will determine whether you can make sense of the result

**Before casting:** State your inquiry aloud, or hold it clearly in mind. Traditional approaches include invoking divinity first (see Invoking Divinity below) and presenting the question as part of that invocation.

## Invoking Divinity (Optional but Traditional)

*(Source: Wen ch. 7)*

In the traditional yarrow stalk method, certain steps explicitly call for invoking Divinity. This step is optional for secular users — at minimum, it is a moment to be earnest, sincere, and humble.

**A commonly used invocation (reworded from Taoist sources):**

> "I call upon the Taiji, the numinous Light, the Wuji, the numinous stillness of Being. I call upon the affinity of the sun and the moon, and the four faces of the holy Spirit, and who, through the five mysteries of change, produce the eight divine revelations. I seek the Spirit's guidance. I seek the Tao. [State your inquiry for divination.]"

The terms in this invocation: Taiji = the divine monad (omnipresent Light of creation); Wuji = numinous void and nothingness; sun and moon = yin and yang; four faces of the holy Spirit = the changing and unchanging lines of yin and yang (elder and younger); five mysteries = Wu Xing five phases of change; eight divine revelations = Ba Gua.

**Taoist invocation of the Lady of the Nine Heavens (Jiǔ Tiān Xuán Nǚ 九天玄女):**

> "Jiǔ Tiān Xuán Nǚ, Lady of the Nine Heavens, Lady of the Mysteries: lend me the spirit and power of your sword, and the spirit and power of your gourd. I seek answers, I seek knowledge. I seek guidance on the auspices of my present Path . . ." [Then state your inquiry.]

**Buddhist approach:** Recite the name of Amitabha (阿彌陀佛 Amítuófó) before commencing, and again at close.

## The Yarrow Stalk Method

*(Primary source: Wen ch. 7, drawing on the Ten Wings / Xici, with Zhu Xi's reconstruction; corroborated in Alfred Huang)*

**Equipment:** 50 yarrow stalks (or bamboo skewers) of equal length; paper and pen.

**Cosmological basis (from the Great Treatise / Ten Wings):**
> The number of the Great Expansion is fifty, of which forty-nine are used. Divide them into two, symbolizing the two primary forces. Suspend one, symbolizing the three supreme powers. Manipulate by four, symbolizing the four seasons. Return the remainder, symbolizing the intercalary month. In five years there is another intercalation. Afterward the process is repeated. Therefore four operations produce a change, and eighteen changes yield a gua.

**Why 50 stalks?** The numbers 1–10 include five odd (Heaven/yang: 1+3+5+7+9 = 25) and five even (Earth/yin: 2+4+6+8+10 = 30); their sum is 55, representing a balance of gods and demons. The 10 ordinals multiplied by 5 (Wu Xing designations) = 50.

**Why 49 used?** Wang Bi interprets the 49 active stalks as the universe of the questioner (Taoist Being). The single unused stalk represents wu (無), Nonbeing — the Taoist principle of wu wei.

### Step-by-Step: One Line (Three Counts)

The full procedure below yields one hexagram line. Repeat the entire procedure six times (from bottom to top) to construct all six lines.

**Gather all 50 stalks.**

**Step 1 — Set one stalk aside (vertical).**
As you do this, invoke Divinity and/or state your inquiry for divination. This single stalk represents the Taiji: the beginning, before differentiation of Heaven and Earth. It plays no further part in this round.

**Step 2 — Divide the 49 into two bundles.**
Split the remaining 49 at random — one bundle in each hand. Place one bundle on top of the single vertical stalk (this bundle = Heaven). Set the other bundle at the bottom (this bundle = Earth). You've now connected Heaven and Earth.

**COUNT 1:**

**Step 3 — Take 1 stalk from the bottom Earth bundle.**
Place it between your pinky and ring finger. This stalk = your qi, connecting Heaven and Earth as the diviner.

**Step 4 — Count the bottom Earth bundle in groups of 4.**
Remove groups of 4 until you have 4 or fewer stalks remaining.

**Step 5 — Place this remainder between your ring and middle finger.**

**Step 6 — Count the top Heaven bundle in groups of 4.**
Remove groups of 4 until you have 4 or fewer stalks remaining.

**Step 7 — Place this Heaven remainder between your middle and index finger.**

**Step 8 — Count total stalks in hand.**
You must have either **5 or 9** stalks in your hand (the 1 between pinky/ring + remainder from Earth + remainder from Heaven).

- If 5 stalks → assign the number **3** (5 = terrestrial Wu Xing; converts to 3, the Trinity of Lucks)
- If 9 stalks → assign the number **2** (9 = nine heavenly kingdoms; converts to 2, the divine binary)

**Set the Count 1 result aside. Record your number (2 or 3).**

---

**COUNT 2:**

Gather up the remaining bundle (all stalks except those set aside from Count 1).

Repeat Steps 1–8 above with this smaller bundle.

This time, the result will be **4 or 8** stalks:
- If 4 stalks → assign the number **3** (4 = four seasons and four directions; converts to 3)
- If 8 stalks → assign the number **2** (8 = eight trigrams; converts to 2)

**Set the Count 2 result aside. Record your number (2 or 3).**

---

**COUNT 3:**

Gather the remaining bundle. Repeat Steps 1–8.

Again, the result will be **4 or 8**:
- If 4 stalks → assign the number **3**
- If 8 stalks → assign the number **2**

**Record your number (2 or 3).**

---

### Reading the Line from the Three Counts

Add the three assigned numbers together. The total sum determines the line value:

| Sum | Line Type | Character | Name |
|-----|-----------|-----------|------|
| 6 = 2+2+2 | Changing yin (X) | — — with mark | Younger yin (少陰) |
| 7 = 2+2+3 or 2+3+2 or 3+2+2 | Unchanging yang | —— | Elder yang (太陽) |
| 8 = 2+3+3 or 3+2+3 or 3+3+2 | Unchanging yin | — — | Elder yin (太陰) |
| 9 = 3+3+3 | Changing yang (X) | —— with mark | Younger yang (少陽) |

- **Changing lines** (6 or 9) are marked with an X and will transform into their opposite in the derived hexagram
- **Unchanging lines** (7 or 8) stay the same in both hexagrams

### Building the Hexagram

- Record lines **from bottom to top** (line 1 at the bottom, line 6 at the top)
- Repeat all three counts six times to produce all six lines
- Lines 1–3 = lower trigram (inner gua); Lines 4–6 = upper trigram (outer gua)
- Look up the hexagram by cross-referencing the upper and lower trigrams

### Note on Probabilities

The mathematical probability of each sum with yarrow stalks is:
- 6 (changing yin): 1/16
- 7 (unchanging yang): 7/16
- 8 (unchanging yin): 7/16
- 9 (changing yang): 1/16

This produces a bias toward unchanging lines, making changing lines relatively rare in the yarrow stalk method.

## Alfred Huang's Simplified Yarrow Stalk Method

*(Source: Alfred Huang, The Complete I Ching, lines 564–581)*

This simplified version, handed down from Master Yin, produces one moving line with certainty:

**To obtain the lower trigram:**
1. Hold 50 bamboo sticks in your left hand
2. Place one aside on the table in front of you, parallel to your body
3. Randomly divide the remaining 49 into two groups on the table, left and right
4. Take one stick from the right pile; place it between your ring finger and little finger
5. Pick up the right pile; with your right hand take 4 sticks at a time and put them aside in groups of 8
6. Repeat until 7 or fewer sticks remain in hand
7. Add the remaining sticks to the one between your ring and little finger
8. The sum = the number of your **lower trigram** (matching it to Fu Xi's Early Heaven sequence)

**Repeat** procedures 1–8 to obtain the **upper trigram**.

**To obtain the moving line:**
- Repeat procedures 1–5
- After picking up the left group, take **6 sticks** at a time (instead of 4) and put them aside
- Repeat until 5 or fewer sticks remain
- Add the remainder to the 1 between your fingers
- The sum = the **moving line number** (counted up from the bottom)

## The Three-Coin Method

*(Primary sources: Wen ch. 7, Alfred Huang; both traditions covered)*

**History:** Records of coins in I Ching divination date to before AD 200. The three-coins-tossed-six-times method gained popularity during the Tang dynasty (AD 618–907). Traditional Chinese coins for divination have a circular form (representing Heaven) with a square cutout at the center (representing Earth) — an embodiment of "circling the square."

**Equipment:** 3 coins of the same denomination; paper and pen.

**Two conflicting conventions exist:**

| Result | Image & Number Tradition | Meaning & Principle Tradition |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| 3 tails | Fixed yin line | **Changing yin line (X)** |
| 2 tails + 1 head | Changing yin line (X) | **Fixed yin line** |
| 2 heads + 1 tail | Changing yang line (X) | **Fixed yang line** |
| 3 heads | Fixed yang line | **Changing yang line (X)** |

**The Meaning and Principle tradition** (the majority view; followed in Wen source): Three identical results (3 heads or 3 tails) = changing line; mixed results (2+1) = fixed line. Rationale: the probability of getting all 3 the same is 1 in 7; the probability of a mixed result is 3 in 7 — so the rarer, more extreme result produces the changing line.

**The Image and Number tradition** assigns younger yin and younger yang (the fluctuating, shifting energies) to produce changing lines. Elder yin and elder yang are matured and therefore fixed.

*Both approaches coexist in classical and modern texts. Choose one and apply it consistently.*

### Step-by-Step (Meaning & Principle Tradition)

1. **Formulate your question.** Hold it clearly in mind; envision a conversation with the Book.
2. **Hold the three coins** in your cupped hands; shake them while focusing on your inquiry.
3. **Let the coins land** on a flat surface; observe the result.
4. **Read the result** using the table above; record the line (solid yang or broken yin, with X mark if changing).
5. Lines are constructed **bottom to top** — the first toss = line 1 (bottom).
6. **Repeat** for a total of **six tosses** to construct all six lines.
7. Lines 1–3 = lower trigram; lines 4–6 = upper trigram.
8. Use the trigram cross-reference table to identify both the **primary hexagram** and (if any changing lines) the **transformed hexagram**.

**Marking changing lines:** Mark changing lines with an X in the column between the primary and transformed hexagram columns on your record sheet.

## Other Casting Methods

### James Legge's Simplified Coin Toss (1899)
*(Source: Wen ch. 5, Practicum 5.12)*

Take three pennies and throw them together a total of six times. Interpret results per Legge's table (Image and Number tradition assignment):
- 3 tails → Fixed yin
- 1 head + 2 tails → Changing yin
- 2 heads + 1 tail → Changing yang
- 3 heads → Fixed yang

### Bibliomancy / Plum Blossom Method (梅花易數)
*(Source: Wen ch. 4, Practicum 4.2; ch. 7)*

Devised by Shao Yong (邵雍, 1011–1077). No physical stalks or coins needed — numbers derived from date, time, or random numbers. Steps:

1. Open the I Ching to a random page; note the page number → this yields the **lower trigram** (divide by 8; take the remainder, or the number itself if ≤8)
2. Open to a second random page; note the page number → this yields the **upper trigram**
3. Open to a third random page; divide that page number by 6; note the **remainder** → this is the **changing line** (if the page divides evenly by 6, the changing line is line 6)

### Eight-Coin Magic Method
*(Source: Alfred Huang)*

Uses 8 coins of the same size, with one coin marked (a dot on the tail side):
1. Mix the 8 coins with tails face down; cover each circle around a Ba Gua chart (1→4 counterclockwise, 5→8 clockwise)
2. Turn coins over in sequence; the dotted coin indicates the **lower trigram**
3. Repeat to obtain the **upper trigram**
4. Remove 2 unmarked coins; mix the remaining 6 with tails face down; place vertically from bottom to top
5. Turn coins over one by one; the dotted coin's position = the **moving line**

### Eight Gemstone / Card Augur Method
*(Source: Alfred Huang)*

Each of the 8 primary gua is assigned to a gemstone or card. To divine:
1. Draw one gemstone from the bag → **lower trigram**
2. Return it; draw again → **upper trigram**
3. Roll a die → **moving line** (counting from the bottom)

### Rice Grain Method
*(Source: Wen ch. 7, Practicum 7.7; ch. 4, Practicum 4.4)*

Scoop a spoonful of uncooked rice grains (or dry beans). Count the grains; divide by 8 to find the remainder → this yields a trigram or hexagram number. A third scooping (divided by 6) yields the changing line.

### Cowrie Shell Method
*(Source: Wen ch. 7, Practicum 7.6)*

Toss a set of cowrie shells; read the pattern of open/closed faces to determine yin/yang values for each line. See Wen source for the cowrie shell reference table.

### Channeling / Mediumship Method
*(Source: Wen ch. 7, Practicum 7.10)*

After entering an altered state of consciousness (meditation, invocation), the diviner compulsively marks one of the six lines of a hexagram as the changing line. This method relies entirely on psychic attunement rather than mathematical casting.

## Recording a Hexagram

- **Always write hexagrams from bottom to top:** Line 1 is at the bottom; Line 6 is at the top
- Mark changing lines with an **X** immediately to the side of the line
- Record both the **primary hexagram** (the one you cast) and the **transformed hexagram** (produced by changing lines)
- Name your upper and lower trigrams; use the trigram cross-reference table to find the hexagram number

**Standard notation:**
- Solid line (——) = yang line
- Broken line (— —) = yin line
- Solid line with circle (——○) = changing yang (value 9 in yarrow stalk method)
- Broken line with cross (— —×) = changing yin (value 6 in yarrow stalk method)

## Moving Lines — What They Are and What They Mean

**What makes a line moving:**

In the yarrow stalk method:
- A sum of **6** = changing yin (younger yin, 少陰) — marked with X
- A sum of **9** = changing yang (younger yang, 少陽) — marked with X

In the coin method (Meaning & Principle tradition):
- **3 tails** = changing yin
- **3 heads** = changing yang

**What a moving line signifies:**

A moving line represents **the present stage of development** in the circumstances inquired about. It indicates the specific moment within the larger situation where you are currently positioned. If there are no moving lines, conditions are static or fixed. Moving lines are where transformation is occurring.

Changing lines transform into their polar opposite in the derived hexagram:
- A changing yang line (——) becomes a yin line (— —) in the transformed hexagram
- A changing yin line (— —) becomes a yang line (——) in the transformed hexagram

**Greater and lesser yin/yang:**
- Elder yang (太陽) and Elder yin (太陰) are stable, matured forces → unchanging (fixed) lines
- Younger yang (少陽) and Younger yin (少陰) are fluctuating, shifting forces → changing lines

**Alchemical symbolism of the four line types (Image and Number tradition):**
- Elder yin: Blackening / nigredo — entering the dark chaos of the inner world; catharsis; long window of opportunity to change course
- Younger yin: Whitening / albedo — contemplating the "why"; purification; movement of forces is toward yin
- Younger yang: Yellowing / citrinitas — actualizing self-awareness; new lease on life; movement of forces is toward yang
- Elder yang: Reddening / rubedo — full manifestation and completion

## Primary Hexagram vs. Derived (Transformed) Hexagram

**The primary hexagram** describes your present situation — the current field of energy, the circumstances, the overall context of the inquiry.

**The transformed (derived) hexagram** is produced by changing all moving lines to their opposites:
- All fixed lines remain the same in both hexagrams
- All changing lines flip to their opposite (yang → yin or yin → yang)

**If there are no changing lines** (a locked hexagram): The outcome is relatively fixed. All possible variables that would have determined the outcome are already known. Read the hexagram as expressing either a complete situation or an internal stasis that must first be resolved from within.

**Approached gua (Alfred Huang's term):** The hexagram formed after a moving line alternates. It indicates one's future tendency or potential.

## Multiple Moving Lines — Resolving Complexity

When multiple moving lines appear, their texts may conflict. Alfred Huang's method (handed down from Master Yin):

- **Two moving lines — one yin, one yang:** Consult only the **yin moving line**
- **Two moving lines — both yin or both yang:** Consult only the **lower one**
- **Three moving lines:** Consult only the **middle one**
- **Four moving lines:** Consult only the **upper of the two non-moving lines**
- **Five moving lines:** Consult only the **other, non-moving line**
- **All six lines moving:** Consult the Decision of the **transformed hexagram**

Special case: For Hexagram 1 (Qian) and Hexagram 2 (Kun), which each have a seventh invisible line, consult the special seventh Yao Text (called "All Nines" or "All Sixes").

## The Interpretation Process

*(Sources: Wen ch. 7 "Summary Outline of Hexagram Interpretation"; Alfred Huang)*

### Step 1 — Read the Primary Hexagram's Oracle / Decision
The Oracle message (or King Wen's Decision, the Tuan text) is a brief recapitulation of the divinatory message — a concise summary of the situation. Read it as a riddle to solve. It denotes a general prognostication (auspicious vs. inauspicious; take action or do not).

> Confucius: "The wise person, just by contemplating the decision of the gua, is capable of realizing most of the content."

Do not ignore the name and symbol of the hexagram — this is the root. Many beginners focus on the line texts while neglecting the broader situation; this is "seeing the trees and missing the forest."

### Step 2 — Read the Changing Lines (if any)
The changing lines convey **concrete points of advice** from the Oracle. They are action items — your to-do list. They give practical advice on how to navigate the situation and how to turn the tides in your favor.

- A changing line indicates forces still in motion — you still have opportunity to influence the outcome
- The more changing lines, the more volatile the situation — more variables are at play, but also more opportunity for intervention

**Changing line position correspondences (Wen source, Table 7-19):**
- Line 6: Greater spiritual forces, karma, or destiny. Acts of God or forces beyond your control. Transition phase; one chapter ending, another beginning.
- Line 5 (Hexagram ruler): Executive leadership or decision-making is needed. What's to come will have long-term implications.
- Line 4: Interpersonal relationships, matters of the heart, emotional responses, or family matters. Decisive action may be needed now; time is of the essence.
- Line 3: Legal matters, civil affairs, or social affairs are key factors. Plot thickens; complications; oppositional forces are most impactful.
- Line 2: Physical health, medical, or technological factors. Situation is gaining momentum — blooming and developmental phase.
- Line 1: Short-term financial or economic factors. Situation is still at the onset; initial setup. Decisions will have only short-term implications.

### Step 3 — Consider the Trigram Correspondences
Study the two primary gua (lower and upper trigrams) that constitute the hexagram. Note:
- What do their attributes and qi qualities suggest about the inner and outer situations?
- What is the Wu Xing phase of each trigram, and what is the alchemical interaction between them?
- Which trigram is dominant; which is subservient?
- Confucius's Commentary on the Symbol explains the significance of the hexagram in terms of the two primary gua

### Step 4 — Read the Transformed Hexagram (if changing lines exist)
The transformed hexagram reads like an **if-then statement** — revealing what could happen if the advice of the Oracle is not heeded, or alternatively, the potential outcome if it is followed.

Read: (a) the Oracle message of the transformed hexagram, and (b) the line statements at the positions of the changing lines in the transformed hexagram.

### Step 5 — Examine the Mutual / Nuclear Hexagram (Optional, Advanced)
The nuclear hexagram (formed by lines 2–4 as the lower nuclear trigram and lines 3–5 as the upper nuclear trigram) reveals the hidden, inner meaning. Reading its name, Decision, and Commentary illuminates latent dynamics.

### Step 6 — Synthesize
Reflect on all elements: the Oracle summary, the changing line advice, the trigram dynamics, the transformed hexagram outcome, any nuclear insights. Integrate them as a coherent narrative about the situation.

## How to Approach a Locked Hexagram (No Changing Lines)

A locked hexagram has no changing lines — all tosses or counts produced fixed (unchanging) lines.

**Two interpretive approaches:**
1. **The outcome is relatively fixed:** All variables that would determine the outcome are already known; no conditional branches remain. Read as describing a complete, defined situation with no immediately mutable factors.
2. **Internal stasis:** Something within must first be unblocked. There is an internalized stalemate; no actions are being taken to move the situation along. The inner dynamics must resolve first before outer movement is possible.

In both cases: read all six line statements as a **narrative arc** — a field to scry into and navigate. The line position correspondences (Line 1 = onset; Line 5 = ruler/apex; Line 6 = transition/completion) still apply. Focus on the hexagram ruler (line 5) as the prognostication for what is to come when no changing lines are present.

## Why Divination Works — Two Frameworks

**Taoist / Traditional Chinese perspective:** The yarrow stalks respond to the questioner as echoes of the spirits governing that change. Every hexagram emanates a particular qi life force (卦氣, guà qì) that influences the human condition. Divination detects what qi and change-process is currently at work, and presents the result as a six-line coded diagram (Wang Bi). At no time should divination be construed as removing human agency.

**Jungian / Psychological perspective (Wilhelm / Jung):** The casting process is representative of the inquirer's present space and moment in time. "The manipulation of the yarrow stalks makes it possible for the unconscious in man to become active" (Wilhelm). The I Ching acts as a clock or calendar — an algorithmic wisdom forecasting what is to come. The effort to interpret symbols drawn by a randomized process is essentially a form of free association, revealing latent issues as in dream interpretation.

Both frameworks agree: **the answer lies in the quality of engagement** — sincerity, preparation, and genuine reflection on the Oracle's message.

## Reading Both Hexagrams Together

*(Source: Wen ch. 7, Bel Yong personal approach)*

One practitioner's synthesis approach:

1. The Oracle message of the **primary hexagram** = a summary of the situation; the thesis (like the abstract of an article)
2. The **changing lines** in the primary hexagram = practical advice and forecasts based on current variables in play
3. The **transformed hexagram** Oracle message = the if-then statement; what could happen if you do not heed the Oracle's advice
4. The **changing lines in the transformed hexagram** = parting words; final thoughts on the situation

This produces a complete narrative: present situation → advice → conditional future → closing counsel.

## Reading Line Positions (Structural Significance)

*(Source: Alfred Huang, glossary)*

The six positions in any hexagram carry inherent meanings regardless of yin/yang content:

- **Line 1 (Initial):** Someone about to enter a situation or with little power over it; beginning stages; foundation
- **Line 2:** Position for an official or general; favorable if not directly controlled by the ruler (line 5); a place of common interest with the ruler
- **Line 3:** Transition from inner to outer situation; insecure; complications and conflicts arise here
- **Line 4:** Position for a minister or adviser; close to the ruler (line 5) — caution required; matters of relationship and the heart
- **Line 5:** Position of the ruler; most often the host of the hexagram; what you can expect in the long term
- **Line 6 (Top):** Position for a sage or hermit; someone retired from human affairs; completion; transition; what lies beyond the current cycle

**Correctness:** A yang line at an odd position (1, 3, 5) is "correct." A yin line at an even position (2, 4, 6) is "correct." Incorrect placement creates tension but not necessarily misfortune.

**Central positions:** The 2nd and 5th places are central (middle of each trigram) — generally most favorable. The 5th place is superior.
