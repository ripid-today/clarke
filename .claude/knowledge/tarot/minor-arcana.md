# Tarot — Minor Arcana (Marseille Tradition)
<!-- sources: 08_marseille_tarot.md -->
<!-- domain: tarot -->

## Overview

The Minor Arcana consists of 56 cards divided into four suits of 14 cards each. Each suit contains: one Ace card, nine number cards (2–10), and four court cards (Page, Knight, Queen, King). This structure closely parallels ordinary playing cards, with the addition of the Page rank.

All card descriptions refer to the CBD Tarot de Marseille (restoration of the Nicolas Conver 1760 deck by Yoav Ben-Dov).

## Soft Suits vs. Hard Suits

**Soft suits** (Coins and Cups): Receptive, feminine, favorable. Round and enclosed symbolic objects (coin, cup). Number cards colored mainly yellow with some red — warm and pleasant, suggesting gold and emotional warmth. Objects suggest containment and conservation.

**Hard suits** (Wands and Swords): Outgoing, masculine, challenging. Long, narrow symbolic objects that can serve as weapons. Number cards colored in red and black, with long parallel interlocking bars suggesting struggle, closure, and entanglement.

---

## Interpreting Number Cards

### Two methods:
1. **By table:** Cross the numerological meaning of the card number with the suit domain (e.g., 3 of Coins = dynamism/movement in the practical/material domain).
2. **By image:** Notice the arrangement of suit symbols, the plant decorations, and the geometric patterns for an intuitive story.

### Directional symbolism in number cards:
- The **vertical axis** is the primary axis in number cards (unlike the horizontal axis dominant in major suit and court cards)
- **Bottom to top** = time progression from past/early stage to future/later stage
- **Top to bottom** (soft suits) = power and status hierarchy (top = superior/management, bottom = subordinate)

## SUIT OF COINS (Deniers)
**Element:** Earth | **Domain:** Body / Material / Physical | **Direction:** North | **Body Part:** Legs

**Suit character:** Stable and conservative, like the round coin enclosed in itself. Practical thinking; preference for concrete and tangible things; preservation of what already exists. Related to money, workplace, the body, health, food, and physical space.

**Number card design:** Coin icons are not labeled with a number — one must count them. All coins in number cards (except 2 of Coins) share the same design: a flat circle with a fourfold flower-like decoration. They are arranged in symmetrical pairs, creating solid, conservative structures.

---

### Ace of Coins
**Visual:** The coin lies flat as if on the ground (unlike the other aces which stand upright). A large four-petaled flower at its center; elaborate plant decorations above and below, still turned inward. Largely symmetrical between straight and inverse.

**Interpretation (upright and inverse similar):** A good start in material things. Financial and physical stability. A practical perspective. A significant sum of money. Utilitarian approach. Something basic and unsophisticated. Greed. The coin as foundation from which other suits grow — it contains the potential for the whole deck within its four-petaled center.

---

### 2 of Coins
**Visual:** Two large, highly detailed coins connected by a snake-like or ribbon band. The card contains text with the publisher's name, year, and place of publication (traditional in Marseille decks). The coins are the largest and most detailed in the entire suit.

**Interpretation:** Duality. Two options or two elements in relationship — collaborating while maintaining distance. A winding road advancing in complex ways. Recognition and acknowledgment. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 3 of Coins
**Visual:** A new, smaller coin appears to emerge from the alliance of the pair below, forming a triangle.

**Interpretation:** Product. A partnership or alliance bears fruit. First results of a project. Good prospects. **Inverse:** Disappointment. A partnership does not bear the expected fruits.

---

### 4 of Coins
**Visual:** Four coins in a square array; a royal shield (Bourbon fleur-de-lys) appears at the center.

**Interpretation:** Stability. Solid material assets. Time-tested reliability. Tradition, honor, and reputation. Established social institutions. **Inverse:** Conservatism. Clinging to old and outdated patterns.

---

### 5 of Coins
**Visual:** A new coin appears in the center, disrupting the even square arrangement.

**Interpretation:** Disruption. Something new appears and destabilizes existing structures. A new element gets attention but also awakens resistance. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 6 of Coins
**Visual:** A more flexible, rounded, and dynamic arrangement departing from stacked pairs. Harmonious grouping.

**Interpretation:** Expansion. Abundance of resources and possible ways to advance. A positive outlook; success. A good balance between stability and movement. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 7 of Coins
**Visual:** A single coin in the middle of a layered-pairs structure. Plant decorations around it suggest support and integration.

**Interpretation:** Acceptance. Something new is well received. Help and protection. Integrating into a system without losing one's individuality. (Can also represent one's individual position within a larger organization.) **Inverse:** Lack of independence; need to rely on others' help and acceptance.

---

### 8 of Coins
**Visual:** A complex, hierarchical, mechanically uniform structure. Can remind one of an office building or organization chart.

**Interpretation:** Uniformity. A mechanical structure. Practical considerations prove efficient but lack a human touch. Routine work. A slow and patient advance. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 9 of Coins
**Visual:** A single coin carved into a narrow, contained central space.

**Interpretation:** Motivation. Carving a niche for oneself in an existing system. Ambition. Endurance and independent thinking bear long-term fruits. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 10 of Coins
**Visual:** Unique design for the suit; high number of coins in an intensive arrangement.

**Interpretation:** Abundance. Intensive activity in practical affairs. Material success and achievements. Some may be getting more than others. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### Page of Coins (Valet de Deniers)
**Visual:** A young man looks intently at a coin in his raised hand. A second coin lies near his foot, unnoticed. His feet point in opposite directions. His title is written down the side of the card (not in the usual bottom strip).

**Interpretation:** A practical endeavor. Untapped potentials are within reach. Tangible success at the beginning stage. A solid material base for further advancement. Limited vision focused only on what is already in hand. **Inverse:** Hesitation, lack of clear purpose. Thinking in terms of past achievements misses present opportunities.

---

### Knight of Coins (Cavalier de Deniers)
**Visual:** A knight mounted on a horse, moving right, gazes at a coin in front of him. The coin's slices seem to tilt as if rolling away. The knight holds a green wand, hinting at the wands suit.

**Interpretation:** Advancing toward a tangible goal that seems close but somehow evades actual grasp. Aspiration to combine creativity and material consideration. Personal impulses might interfere with practical plans. Constant chasing after money. **Inverse:** Passions and desires interfere with practical plans; never reaching material stability.

---

### Queen of Coins (Reyne de Deniers)
**Visual:** A mature woman on a throne, gazing left at the large, focused coin she holds (the largest and most developed coin in the suit, with a central focal point). The scepter in her other hand forms a triangle with the coin's center.

**Interpretation:** Tangible assets; material and personal stability; a sober, realistic vision. Pragmatic perspective. Feeling at ease with one's assets and position. **Inverse:** Conservatism; resistance to change; looking at things only from the material perspective.

---

### King of Coins (Roy de Deniers)
**Visual:** Seated in an open, natural landscape (unlike the other kings in artificial environments). No visible crown (implicit in yellow triangles on hat). Holds his coin casually in his lap. His gaze is open toward the future. His posture leans slightly back — cautious, not rushing. His legs are crossed (like the Emperor), suggesting self-discipline.

**Interpretation:** Confidence and security; cautious but optimistic vision. Looking for new achievements while keeping existing assets secure. Self-control, maturity, and responsibility. Modest and unpretentious behavior. **Inverse:** Dissatisfaction with what one has. Disregard of present good things. A limited outlook.

---

## SUIT OF CUPS (Coupes)
**Element:** Water | **Domain:** Emotion / Feeling / Relationships | **Direction:** West | **Body Part:** Chest

**Suit character:** Romantic, nostalgic, spiritual. The cup is a receptacle that serves for something else — its content. May be filled with water (life-giving flow), wine (joy, illusions), poison (negative feelings, hatred), or empty (emotional block). Emotion, human relations, and mystical feelings of devotion. The nature of the suit suggests things beyond the tangible world.

**Number card design:** Cup icons appear in various arrangements. The 2 of Cups is distinctive with elaborate detail, containing heraldic symbols and fantastical fish-head decorations at the top. When cups appear inverse, the icons turn upside down — a significant visual change.

---

### Ace of Cups
**Visual:** A large goblet with a hexagonal base and wide ring around its stem. Three jagged light-blue shapes at the middle may be water streams or wings. Vertical structures at the upper edges resemble cathedral towers pointing skyward but not quite reaching the card edge. The base seems to emerge from a water-covered surface.

**Interpretation (upright):** A new love. Intimacy, mystery, warm hidden feelings. Focusing on romantic or emotional issues. Yearning for someone absent or longing for something unattainable. A romantic, spiritual, or idealistic attitude. Devotion and submission to something beyond yourself. **Inverse:** Emotional dryness, feeling empty. Avoidance of intimacy. Negative feelings. Heartbreak.

---

### 2 of Cups
**Visual:** Two detailed cups with fantastical animal (fish) faces above them. A crown with decorative branches appears at the bottom. Heraldic shield with three fleurs-de-lys. The letters "G" and "M" appear. This is the most elaborate of the cups number cards.

**Interpretation:** Partnership. A romantic relationship or close personal alliance. Interpersonal dynamics based on social norms. Passion in a love relationship that may turn against itself. **Inverse:** A crisis in a couple relationship. Disappointment with someone close.

---

### 3 of Cups
**Visual:** Two cups at the bottom (a pair of lovers); a heart-shaped arrangement of leaves around a red central form; a third cup emerges above.

**Interpretation:** Birth. Something new brings joy and happiness. Caring for a child. Issues of child-parent relations. A common project motivated by feelings and not only by interests. **Inverse:** Problems in relations with one's parents or child. A strong alliance of two persons leaves a third outside.

---

### 4 of Cups
**Visual:** Four cups arranged in a stable square configuration.

**Interpretation:** Family. A collective of people (family, community) with a history and a sense of belonging. Commitment to a group at the price of giving up personal interests. **Inverse:** Problems and discord in the family or long-lasting community. A fixed social structure not allowing adaptation.

---

### 5 of Cups
**Visual:** A central cup surrounded by four others with plants and flowers streaming toward it and from it.

**Interpretation:** Links. Popularity; relations with many people. Becoming the center of attention in a group. Relying on connections with others to advance or overcome difficulties. **Inverse:** Excessive preoccupation with social activity. Losing oneself in superficial connections. Cultivating virtual instead of real contacts.

---

### 6 of Cups
**Visual:** Six cups in a long-term, stable arrangement.

**Interpretation:** Continuity. A long-term relationship. Repetition between different generations in the family. A stable personal alliance. **Inverse:** Monotony, tedious repetition. Falling time and again into the same emotional traps.

---

### 7 of Cups
**Visual:** A single individual cup in a distinctive position relative to the grouped others.

**Interpretation:** Individuality. A single person finding their place in a group. Contact with people in high positions. Exceptional qualities are appreciated. **Inverse:** Problems of integration. Being part of a collective but feeling isolated and estranged.

---

### 8 of Cups
**Visual:** A developed arrangement of cups with interactive, involved groupings.

**Interpretation:** Involvement. Developing personal relationships within a group. A favorable human-relations environment. A feast or family event. **Inverse:** Interference of the environment in a couple's relationship. Family pressures in romantic matters.

---

### 9 of Cups
**Visual:** Cups arranged cooperatively, each in a proper place.

**Interpretation:** Coordination. People or parts working together, each in its proper place. Accepting one's role in a social or group environment. Happiness. Wishes coming true. **Inverse:** A confusing social situation. Difficulty situating oneself in a complex environment.

---

### 10 of Cups
**Visual:** One large cup on top of nine smaller ones — a leadership structure visible at a glance.

**Interpretation:** Leadership. A person with special qualities receives appreciation and high status. Assuming responsibility for others. Maintaining a superior position. **Inverse:** A fallen leader. Loss of popularity. Disappointment due to ingratitude from people one has helped.

---

### Page of Cups (Valet de Coupes)
**Visual:** A young man gazes into his narrow cup, focusing only on the upper portion without penetrating the deep layers. His other hand holds what may be a hat or lid. A cloth covers the cup's back. He moves toward the left; his left foot stumbles on the uneven ground. A hidden dagger is visible on his belt.

**Interpretation:** A fresh, inexperienced attitude in emotional matters. Sentimentality without deep self-understanding. An attempt at self-examination that remains superficial and private. Hesitant steps in the romantic or erotic domain. **Inverse:** Overabsorption in personal feelings. Losing contact with others. Sloppiness in practical affairs.

---

### Knight of Cups (Cavalier de Coupes)
**Visual:** A knight holds out a flat, wide, open cup in the palm of his hand as if presenting it in a romantic gesture. Modest, natural clothing. The cup seems almost to float unsupported. He moves toward the left.

**Interpretation:** Devotion, emotional giving, openness, sincerity. Giving up personal goals to serve something one cares for. A romantic gesture; a potential lover may appear. Open expression of emotions, though perhaps at a shallow level. **Inverse:** Superficial and unstable feelings. Overly optimistic but unrealistic attitude. Insincere feelings displayed.

---

### Queen of Cups (Reyne de Coupes)
**Visual:** A mature woman on a throne, holding the only closed cup in the entire suit. A tight belt covers her chest. Elaborate headgear. A narrow opening allows only a restricted field of vision. She holds a scepter that looks like a sword in her other hand. The canopy leaves only a narrow front aperture.

**Interpretation:** A rich but hidden inner world. Stunted emotion controlled by intellect. Closing in and defending oneself; difficulty opening emotionally. A person with much to offer — one must make an effort to reach them. Guarding something of value. **Inverse:** Closure, defensiveness. Distrust due to negative past experiences. Hiding emotions under rational criticism.

---

### King of Cups (Roy de Coupes)
**Visual:** An older, bearded king seated with a cup that looks slightly divided — as if a broken heart held together. His hat brim is raised, opening his field of vision forward. His feet point left (past), but floor lines converge toward the future (right).

**Interpretation:** Emotional maturity and the wisdom of experience. Ability to overcome past sorrows and look forward. A lucid, realistic outlook that remains positive. Restrained and careful expression of emotions. Openness toward new experiences but with prudence and caution. **Inverse:** Difficulty overcoming an emotional blow. A pessimistic outlook from negative past experiences.

---

## SUIT OF WANDS (Batons)
**Element:** Fire | **Domain:** Desire / Creativity / Sexuality | **Direction:** South | **Body Part:** Pelvis

**Suit character:** Passionate, outgoing, energetic, creative, and sometimes conflictive. Expresses sexuality, growth, self-expression, and giving birth to new things. Relates to creative impulse, stamina, energy, desire to advance and dominate. Can indicate complications, struggles, and difficulties. Originally linked to polo sticks (from Mamluk playing cards) — games, recreation, competition.

**Two shapes of the wand:**
- **Natural green branch** (Ace and court cards): Growth, vitality, creative potential
- **Straight rod with wide blades at each tip** (number cards): Conflict, difficulty, artificial structure

**Number card design:** Cards colored mainly in red and black. Rods appear as straight, narrow stripes with wide blades at the tips, crossing each other. The arrangement creates a sense of struggle, blocking, and entanglement.

---

### Ace of Wands
**Visual:** A large hand emerges from a yellow-tipped shape holding a green wand that is wider at the top (open at the tip, suggestive of a female sexual organ). Red-tipped cut stubs of branches appear along the wand. Drops of color scatter at the sides. The grip is gentler than in the Ace of Swords.

**Interpretation (upright):** A period of growth, drive, and energy; moving forward with passion. Start of a creative project or generally creative period. Dissipation of resources in all directions. Active sexual life. Attempting to advance in multiple directions simultaneously, letting results decide. Acting on desire and impulse. **Inverse:** Restriction, creative block, lack of energy, repressed sexuality.

---

### 2 of Wands
**Visual:** Two crossed rods forming an X.

**Interpretation:** Crossroads. Several options to choose from. Every course offers benefits. A brief encounter with someone going their own way. Blocking an opponent's line of advance. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 3 of Wands
**Visual:** Three rods — one central, two crossing it — suggesting a momentary decision point or middle path.

**Interpretation:** Direction. Moving forward after a moment of hesitation. Finding a middle path between two courses of action. Gaining an advantage by keeping neutrality between two conflicting sides. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 4 of Wands
**Visual:** Four rods in a closed, tense arrangement — a stalemate pattern.

**Interpretation:** Stalemate. A temporary stop to prepare for future advancement. Tensions at present but good prospects in the long run. Making a move now is in nobody's interest. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 5 of Wands
**Visual:** Five rods, with the central one positioned to break through or overcome the crossing pattern.

**Interpretation:** Overcoming. Getting over a weak opposition. Breakdown of equilibrium. Focusing on the main objective. An initiative to make a winning move. **Inverse:** Walking into a complex situation, losing one's edge.

---

### 6 of Wands
**Visual:** Six rods in a strong collaborative arrangement with decorative elements suggesting luxury.

**Interpretation:** Collaboration. A strong alliance between two parties with different goals but common present interests. A taste for luxury made possible by favorable conditions. **Inverse:** Excessive pursuit of luxury. Need to break up an alliance of opponents.

---

### 7 of Wands
**Visual:** Seven rods — one central wand facing many crossing wands — a single fighter against multiple opponents.

**Interpretation:** Struggle. Someone putting up a fight against many opponents. Obstinacy, endurance, keeping one's position in a conflict. A difficult combat with an uncertain outcome. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 8 of Wands
**Visual:** Eight rods in a tightly regulated arrangement with little room for movement.

**Interpretation:** Regulation. Possible to advance only by following the rules. Occupation with short-term goals while losing the long-term perspective. A roadblock. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 9 of Wands
**Visual:** Nine rods arranged so that advance is interrupted by crossing patterns.

**Interpretation:** Interruption. Difficulties and oppositions too hard to overcome. Giving up one's projects to avoid conflict. Starting anew after a challenging period. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 10 of Wands
**Visual:** Ten rods in a pattern suggesting endurance and partnership through hardship.

**Interpretation:** Loyalty. A partnership or alliance endures hardships and succeeds. Pure intentions and perseverance lead to success. Honoring one's principles despite difficulties. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### Page of Wands (Valet de Batons)
**Visual:** A young man stands before a massive natural branch (too large for him to hold comfortably). His hands are posed on it in an awkward, cubist way. The wand seems to absorb his hands rather than he holding it. His feet touch the card's bottom line suggesting a stable base, but the wand is rooted in the ground and may block his advance.

**Interpretation:** Creative potential that still needs processing. Desires and drives not yet controllable. Keeping a safe distance; waiting for the right moment. Tasks too heavy for one's current strength. Using sexuality as a barrier against emotional relationship. **Inverse:** Difficulty controlling desires and urges. Immature approach to sexuality.

---

### Knight of Wands (Cavalier de Batons)
**Visual:** A knight mounted on a heavily covered horse, his body turned left but his face and eyes directed right. He gazes at his wand (more refined than the page's branch but still natural). A floral device on his knee suggests aesthetic sensibility.

**Interpretation:** Suitable resources and impulse to advance, but direction is unclear. Momentary pause to reexamine what one really wants. Excessive preoccupation with one's own sexuality or creative urges. Following instinctual drives may indicate the right direction, but more confidence and commitment are needed. **Inverse:** Preoccupation with desires; problem defining long-term goals; submitting to temptation.

---

### Queen of Wands (Reyne de Batons)
**Visual:** A queen holding a large, semi-elaborate wand like a heavy club; elbows pointing outward express power and dominance. Her look is tender and well-wishing despite the powerful posture. Flowing hair shows sensuality. A wreath of leaves supports her crown (growth and fertility). Her other hand holds something between a fork and a spoon. A single white cuff suggests clean action; her chest is constrained by a belt (emotional block).

**Interpretation:** A strong, self-confident female figure. Gentle speech backed by the presence of a big stick. Using sexuality as a means of power and control. Irresistible drives of passion or creativity. Matters related to food, eating, or cooking. **Inverse:** Intimidation, menace. Fear of feminine power. Problems with a strong mother figure.

---

### King of Wands (Roy de Batons)
**Visual:** An elaborate, artificial wand is held in his right hand (creative energy fully controlled). The right heel is raised, suggesting imminent movement or self-prodding. A pointed triangular shape formed by the wand, leg, and arm almost breaks out of the card frame. His look is cautious, contained between the back of the seat and the hat brim. The wand may replace the missing left column of the chair back — using past resources as a lever for future action.

**Interpretation:** Mature, responsible attitude that harnesses impulses toward creative, controlled action. A look forward with cautious optimism. A moment of prudent consideration before moving in a new direction. Investing accumulated resources to advance rather than merely preserve them. **Inverse:** Plans frustrated by self-defeating acts. Tendency to make things too heavy and complex.

## SUIT OF SWORDS (Epées)
**Element:** Air | **Domain:** Intellect / Rationality / Decisions | **Direction:** East | **Body Part:** Head

**Suit character:** Sharp, determined, calculated, and ready to fight. May also be cold and cruel. Expresses ideas, thoughts, ability to focus, decision making, and the possibility of hurting oneself and others. The word "decision" derives from Latin "cutting off" — choosing one option and cutting off all others.

**Two shapes of the sword:**
- **Straight sword** (Ace, court cards, odd number cards' single sword): Penetration, determination, breaking through
- **Rounded arch-swords** (even number cards, most number cards): Separation, limits, enclosure, blocking

**Number card design:** Cards colored in red and black. Round swords form arched pairs that cross and enclose the center of the card, creating a feeling of blockage, protection, and entanglement.

---

### Ace of Swords
**Visual:** A large straight sword rises prominently through the center. A crown at the top is decorated with two branches (palm and bay laurel — symbols of victory). Red-tipped cut branches suggest sacrifice and hard struggle. A hand emerges from a serrated yellow-edged shape at the lower left — more angular and agitated than in the Ace of Wands.

**Interpretation (upright):** Sharp thinking; a clear definition of goals. Power and control of the situation. An ability to skip deliberation and decide. Cutting away the past, negative influences. A fighting spirit, courage, victory. Separating beneficial from damaging things. Ambition and competitiveness. **Inverse:** Negative and unproductive thoughts. Misconceptions, delusions. Self-defeat. Injury.

---

### 2 of Swords
**Visual:** Two crossed arch-swords forming protective boundaries around a central space. A plant or flower is contained within those boundaries.

**Interpretation:** Boundaries. Limits that protect and define something in development. Making full use of the present situation. Preparations for future advancement. A clear view encompassing the overall situation. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 3 of Swords
**Visual:** One straight sword breaking through the center, flanked by two arch-swords — the single sword wins.

**Interpretation:** Victory. Overcoming a weak opposition. Cutting through a quandary and going forward in a clear direction. A third party intervenes and wins over two weakened opponents. **Inverse:** A failure. Defeat from a weaker opponent. An unsuccessful decisive move.

---

### 4 of Swords
**Visual:** Four arch-swords forming a very tight, restricted enclosure.

**Interpretation:** Restriction. A limited space for development and maneuver. Trying to push against constraints. Potentials to grow once present limitations become less solid. **Inverse:** Confinement and blocking. Lack of motivation or energy to break out of a limited situation.

---

### 5 of Swords
**Visual:** A single straight sword breaking out from the enclosed area.

**Interpretation:** Breakthrough. A forward thrust overcoming the existing limits. Keeping up spirits in a tight situation. Doing things one's own way. **Inverse:** A failed initiative to change the situation. Stubbornness leading nowhere. Oppressing factors cannot be removed now.

---

### 6 of Swords
**Visual:** Six arch-swords in an arrangement suggesting adaptation and order within limits.

**Interpretation:** Adaptation. Accepting limitations and adapting oneself to them. Respecting the present order. Compromising to make the best of the existing situation. **Inverse:** Resignation, surrender, giving up ambition to change things for the better.

---

### 7 of Swords
**Visual:** Seven swords — one prominent central straight sword surrounded by arch-swords — concentrated and focused.

**Interpretation:** Sharpness. A focused and determined attitude. Concentrating on a clear goal and doing what it takes. Winning a fight with evenly balanced odds. **Inverse:** A narrow, overconcentrated vision. Investing in a lost cause.

---

### 8 of Swords
**Visual:** Eight arch-swords arranged as a system of layered defenses and shields.

**Interpretation:** Defenses. Putting up shields and blocks. Psychological defense mechanisms. A need for total control. A well-guarded treasure. Entering another's domain with permission. **Inverse:** Similar.

---

### 9 of Swords
**Visual:** Nine arch-swords with a single element displaying courage — the arrangement suggests imperfect means serving pure intentions.

**Interpretation:** Courage. Winning a fight against a superior force. Pure intentions. Putting imperfect means to good use. **Inverse:** Losing against a stronger opponent. Sloppiness. Imperfect preparations for a challenge.

---

### 10 of Swords
**Visual:** Ten swords in a complex, exhausting arrangement; the two straight swords appear amid the eight arch-swords. Inverse makes the two straight swords feel even more pressing.

**Interpretation:** Exhaustion. A complex situation with many conflicting interests. A long battle without a clear outcome. Need to find an ally attacking the problem from a different direction. **Inverse:** Immobility. Feeling attacked from different sides. A painful and humiliating defeat.

---

### Page of Swords (Valet d'Epées)
**Visual:** A young man holds a large sword resting on his shoulder, blocking his view toward the future. His face turns away from the sword. His feet point in both directions (indecision). He holds what looks like a scabbard in his other hand. A small phallic shape at groin level hints at a dilemma between desire and intellect.

**Interpretation:** Hesitation, indecision. Resources and abilities but not knowing what to do with them. Fear of facing what is coming. The future perceived as a menace. Hindering factors can be turned into useful tools. **Inverse:** Confusion. Negative and inhibiting thoughts. Self-defeat. Sloppy use of one's tools may cause damage.

---

### Knight of Swords (Cavalier d'Epées)
**Visual:** A knight riding emphatically — strong diagonal lines of horse body, sword, and upper silhouette. But he rides toward the left (wrong direction). The horse's forelegs are raised as if refusing to advance. A right-facing mask on the shoulder expresses doubts and second thoughts. White hooves may suggest detachment from the practical ground.

**Interpretation:** Energy, motivation, and resources to advance — but the direction must be checked. Pay attention to hesitation and doubts. Willpower and obstinacy; attempting to impose one's view. Determination may lead to success in an unexpected direction. **Inverse:** Trying to force misguided views. Insisting on a wrong direction. Losing touch with the ground.

---

### Queen of Swords (Reyne d'Epées)
**Visual:** A queen on a throne resembling the Justice card in posture. Two posts behind her with a rounded screen between them. She holds a large sword in her right hand, her gaze directed left — wary of past threats. A rounded abdomen may hint at pregnancy. The sword's hilt doesn't continue exactly the line of the blade, as if holding disconnected parts.

**Interpretation:** A secure and protected but static situation. Entrenching oneself in existing position. Extreme caution, defensiveness, avoidance of risks. Worry about past menaces. Something in preparation kept secret until mature enough to reveal. **Inverse:** Defensiveness and rigidity. Suspicion and fixed ideas block advancement.

---

### King of Swords (Roy d'Epées)
**Visual:** Two shoulder masks express opposing tendencies; a vertical division of the chest suggests a divided heart. Feet drift left, but the sword held in front of the left mask expresses a firm decision to block that direction and look right (future). A strange illustration on the chair base shows uncertainty about the future. A scepter starting at the pelvic area has ruler-like markings. The crown is flooded in light.

**Interpretation:** Willpower and a determined decision to break from past influences and face the future, even with a divided heart. Combination of sharp intelligence and openness to superior wisdom. Readiness to face uncertainty with intellectual tools. Regulated and controlled expression of desires. Making plans and preparations but not yet moving. **Inverse:** A divided heart. Need to cut off from something still held on to. Overcalculating to overcome uncertainty.

## Court Cards: The Four Ranks

### Rank Symbolism

| Rank | French | Visual appearance | Symbolic phase |
|------|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| Page | Valet | Young man, standing, feet on ground | Beginning, hesitation, limited resources, inexperience |
| Knight | Cavalier | Mounted on horse, in open landscape | Movement toward goal, immature abilities, dedication |
| Queen | Reyne | Mature woman, crowned, seated on throne | Realization, enjoying achievements, conservative defense |
| King | Roy | Crowned man, seated, feet visible, unstable posture | Peak maturity, ready to move in new direction |

### The Pages
Young, soft appearance. Standing on untilled ground. Posture expresses hesitation and lack of confidence. Suit symbols appear almost too heavy or complex for them. Represents a person acting in a new field, lacking proficiency, still dependent on others. A fresh approach; readiness to learn.

### The Knights
Mounted on horses in open landscape. Hard-suit knights (wands, swords) wear decorated or armor-like clothing; soft-suit knights (coins, cups) wear simple clothes. Represents advance toward a goal with a sense of mission and dedication. The horse may symbolize those the knight "rides on" (in service) or the means of movement.

### The Queens
Mature women on thrones, wearing crowns. All appear somewhat armed or defensive. Soft-suit queens hold a rod on their left shoulder for protection; hard-suit queens hold their weapons assertively. Expresses achievement and stability, but also conservatism, caution, unwillingness to take risks.

### The Kings
Each wears a hat with an open, upward brim (readiness to receive new ideas). Soft-suit kings are older, bearded, dressed in gowns — mature and cautious wisdom. Hard-suit kings are younger, smooth-faced, wearing armor — action and dynamism. Each king's feet point left (past habits) while his face looks right (future direction). The instability of their throne posture suggests readiness to stand up and move.

## Rank-Suit Cross-Correspondences

The ranks also correspond to the suits:
- Pages → Coins (standing on ground = physical, material)
- Knights → Wands (horse and movement = desire and drive)
- Queens → Cups (feminine, emotional domain)
- Kings → Swords (mature wisdom, decisiveness, intellect)

This gives the suits an ascending order of evolution: Coins (mineral/body) → Wands (vegetable/desire) → Cups (animal/emotion) → Swords (human/intellect).
