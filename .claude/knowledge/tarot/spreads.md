## Overview: The Open Reading Method

The author (Yoav Ben-Dov) presents an approach called the **open reading**, inspired by the teachings of Alejandro Jodorowsky and further developed from personal experience. Three key principles distinguish this approach from conventional methods:

1. **A tarot card has no fixed meaning** that can be learned in advance. The meaning emerges from what we actually see in the card during the reading — its visual impression in context.
2. **The function of each position in a spread is not fixed** in advance. Rather than pre-assigned roles (past/present/future), the role of each card depends on what combination actually appears.
3. **We do not start by interpreting each card separately.** We first try to see the whole picture formed by the cards together, then analyze the role of each element.

## The Reading Session

### The Querent and Reader

The classic reading session involves two participants:
- **The querent** — the one who seeks advice about an issue
- **The reader** — the person who conducts and interprets the cards

Seating arrangement: When the reader and querent are relative strangers, it is better to sit face to face with a low table between them. For people in close relationship, sitting adjacent or side by side may be appropriate.

### The Reading Space

The reading involves a shift in the perception of reality — from ordinary "consensus reality" (where cards are random cardboard) to a reading space where **everything is a sign**.

To mark this transition:
- Clean and prepare a quiet, harmonious space; remove everyday objects
- Use pictures, crystals, plant arrangements, or water vessels with symbolic or spiritual significance
- Light a candle at the beginning and extinguish it at the end (a wooden match is preferred over a lighter)
- Pass the hands over the candle flame to "collect the light"
- Take a moment of quiet self-concentration, meditation, or prayer before beginning
- Optionally: remove shoes when entering the reading space; use incense if both parties appreciate it
- For informal readings (at a party, pub), it is enough to clean a table corner and lay the cards on it

### Handling the Cards

- A new deck should be opened as part of a small ceremony in a quiet place
- Touch the cards one by one to begin a relationship with them; look at the images without haste
- Some readers perform a purification ritual (passing cards through incense smoke or dry sage)
- Keep the deck in a personalized container: a cloth bag (dark blue or soft purple induces calmness) inside a box of organic material (wood, felt, natural fiber), not plastic or animal skin
- A separate reading mat of thick cloth is recommended; use it only for readings

### Session Flow

1. Reader takes out the deck and both take turns shuffling
2. Reader lays cards on the table in the chosen spread layout
3. Reader studies and interprets the cards for the querent
4. Toward the end: check whether the querent has additional questions (re-read existing cards for new questions rather than always opening new ones)
5. Recap and summarize main points and insights
6. Ask the querent for feedback — how they feel, what they understand, what they take away
7. Allow time for an extended ending if needed

## Shuffling the Deck

Two key principles govern shuffling:
1. **The querent's (and reader's) actions determine the choice of cards.** The reader shuffles while listening to the querent's story, then passes the deck for the querent to shuffle. The querent should have the last word.
2. **The choice should be free of deliberate control.** Cards are shuffled facedown so that neither party can consciously choose a specific card.

### Practical methods:
- **Face-to-face:** Reader shuffles facedown while listening, hands to querent who shuffles facedown, querent returns to reader. Cards are drawn from the top (back) of the facedown pack.
- **Fan-method (Marseille major suit only):** Spread the 22 major cards face-down in a fan; the querent picks cards by intuitive feel of the hand.
- **Telephone reading:** Reader shuffles while the querent on the phone tells them when to stop; the top card at each stop point becomes the spread card.
- **Computer reading:** Accepted as legitimate — the computer state at the moment the querent presses "choose" determines the outcome (not random, dependent on querent's action).
- **Inverse cards:** Reader splits the deck, rotates one half 180°, recombines, and shuffles repeatedly. The querent does the same. When the querent returns the cards, the reader inverts the whole deck before drawing, so cards are read from the querent's perspective.

## Formulating the Question

Conventional tarot books attach much importance to explicit question formulation. The open reading treats the querent's stated question as only a starting point.

**Principles:**
- People are not always self-aware enough to know exactly what troubles them
- The real question is not always the stated question
- Be suspicious of an all-too-clear and explicit question — it may be a cover
- Questions formulated as future predictions ("When will I get married?") are actually expressions of present fears. Reframe toward: "What can the querent do now to improve their situation?"
- While the querent speaks, observe: tone of voice, choice of words, degree of openness, body language — all are signs

**Opening questions for the reader to ask the querent:**
- "What brings you here?"
- "What can I do for you?"
- Always use the querent's first name to strengthen the sense of presence

## Everything Is a Sign

A foundational principle: during a reading, **nothing happens by mere chance.** Everything that takes place in and around the reading session may be interpreted as a sign:

- The way the querent presents themselves and their question
- Their tone of voice and choice of words
- How they shuffle (confidently or hesitantly)
- If cards fall from their hands, look at those cards
- If the same cards appear repeatedly across sessions, they may carry a special message
- Body language, sitting position, clothing colors
- Sounds from outside, candle flame movements
- Unusual occurrences at any moment of the reading

## Inverse (Reversed) Cards

### Marseille approach to inversions:
In the Marseille tradition, most cards are not vertically symmetrical (a clear sky-top and earth-bottom), so inversions are meaningful. Several approaches exist:

- **Ignore inversions entirely:** Recommended for beginners; all cards read as upright
- **Inversion as stressing negative aspects:** When a card is inverted, give more weight to its challenging and negative interpretations
- **Visual inversion:** The ground is now on top, weakening the card's openness; the flow lines reverse — for example, the Moon (already heavy) becomes heavier; Force (head controlling lion) becomes lion on top
- **Orientation effect in the spread:** An inverted card's figures face and relate to neighboring cards differently than in the upright position

### Straightening inverse cards during a reading:
The reader can physically straighten an inverse card during the reading to show the querent: "Here is what it looks like when you turn this factor in your favor." This technique reveals what changes the querent can make with resources they already have.

### Inverse note on number cards:
Some number cards are nearly symmetrical — little difference between straight and inverse. Others (especially cups, when upside-down the cup icons invert) show a clear, significant difference. Apply inverse interpretation only when the visual difference is meaningful.

## Reading the Cards: Seeing the Full Picture

### Step 1: The full picture before the details
Before analyzing individual cards, look at the entire spread:
- Let eyes wander over the cards without focusing
- Note the general character and visual pattern of the spread
- Note which cards attract special attention
- Maintain contact with the querent's emotional presence throughout

### Step 2: Human figures
- Where is each figure looking? What direction? What object or figure in its own card or a neighbor?
- Do figures make eye contact or avoid each other's gaze?
- What does their posture and body language express?
- Are they moving toward or away from each other?
- What is their emotional attitude toward each other?

### Step 3: Compositional connections
- Look for lines or surfaces that connect neighboring cards or lead from one to another
- A diagonal line ascending from lower-left to upper-right = positive development
- A diagonal descending from upper-left to lower-right = recession or decline
- Same-color surfaces in neighboring cards may merge into a single shape
- Parallel lines of earth, water, or horizon can flow across multiple cards

### Step 4: Repeating patterns
- Same arrangements of figures in different cards (e.g., two small figures side by side = different phases of the same situation)
- Repeating numbers of objects across cards (three objects in each card = the number three is significant)
- Consecutive card numbers (3, 4, 5 in left-to-right order = ascending process; 5, 4, 3 = decline)
- Similar objects across cards (crowns = domination/wisdom; wands = activity/desire)
- General characteristics shared by all figures (all male, all naked, all holding objects, etc.)

## The Basic Three-Card Spread

**The primary spread of the open reading.** Three cards from the major suit laid down side by side from left to right.

- Can use only the 22 major suit cards (recommended for beginners and experienced readers)
- Read as a story advancing from left to right
- The left card speaks of what came before; the right card of what comes after

### Possible interpretations of the three positions (not fixed):
- **Past / Present / Future** (timeline reading, left to right)
- **Center = the querent; two sides = two paths, options, or influences**
- **Center = main issue; two sides = two aspects of it**
- **Right = active/outgoing factor; left = passive/receiving factor; center = combination or compromise**
- **Center = the querent; right = what helps them; left = what obstructs them**
- **One card may stand out visually (brightest, most dramatic) = the main issue regardless of position**

The open reading does NOT assign a fixed function to each position before the cards are turned. The function emerges from what is actually seen.

### Single card reading:
Used in workshops (each participant picks a card for discussion), or during a session to clarify a specific point. Generally not sufficient alone for a complex personal issue.

## Extensions of the Basic Spread

### The hint card:
The card at the bottom of the deck (visible when the deck is turned over) can be used as a general statement about the query, as a side issue, or as an additional perspective. The reader may glance at it before the spread but introduce it at the right moment.

### Numerological summary card (theosophical addition):
Add the numbers of the cards in the spread. If the total is below 22, the major card with that number becomes the "summary card." If 22 or more, add the digits of the total together (e.g., 10 + 17 + 18 = 45 → 4 + 5 = 9 → The Hermit). If the summary card is already in the spread, that card receives special emphasis.

### Adding cards to extend the reading:
- Add one or more cards to the **right** to see how the story continues
- Add a card to the **left** to see the origin of the situation
- Form a **cross**: add a card above the middle (spiritual aspects / higher meaning) and below the middle (subconscious feelings / emotional base / deep motivating patterns)

## The Row Spread

### Purpose:
A horizontal row spread using all 78 cards (major and minor suits shuffled together). Suitable when the reader knows the minor cards well enough.

### Structure:
- Lay cards side by side, left to right
- Seven cards is recommended as a number for a coherent story with a defined center
- Six cards can be split into two groups of three to compare two options side by side
- Alternatively: lay cards until you feel you have enough (but avoid stopping prematurely at a favorable card)

### Reading approach:
Same as the three-card spread — look for the full picture first, then individual meanings. All cards (major and minor) are read in a continuous story moving left to right.

---

## The Background Layers Spread (Major + Minor Integration)

### Purpose:
Using minor suit cards as a background layer beneath the three-card major suit spread. Suitable for readers with some experience in major cards who are beginning to integrate minor cards.

### Method:
1. Shuffle the complete 78-card deck
2. Draw cards and place them faceup in the left position
3. If the first card is a minor suit card, place a second card on top of it; continue until a major suit card appears on top
4. Repeat for the center and right positions
5. End result: three piles, each topped by a major card, with minor cards beneath

### Three levels of reading:
1. **Level 1:** Read only the three top major cards (the basic spread)
2. **Level 2:** Note the number of cards in each pile (large pile = complicated situation; many cards = many factors), the distribution of suits across the piles, and the types of minor cards present (aces = strong drive; number cards = detailed events; court cards = people or attitudes)
3. **Level 3 (advanced):** Read the minor cards in each pile as a story or process leading up to the major card on top

## The Choice Spread

### Purpose:
Suitable when the querent has to choose between two or more options.

### Method:
1. Explain clearly: the cards will not make the choice; they offer a new perspective
2. Ask the querent to mentally divide the table into two equal parts — one for each option — but not to reveal which part represents which option
3. Hand the querent the shuffled deck; they lay three cards facedown in each part
4. Before turning the cards over, observe how the querent laid them: confidence or hesitation in movement, order of laying, position within the available area (bottom edge = not using full range of opportunity; rising positions = advancement; converging cards = focusing)
5. Share observations and ask the querent which option each part represents
6. Turn the cards over and read each side as a separate spread
7. Pay special attention to parallels and opposing features between the two sides

### Extension:
Can expand to three or more options. The same principle applies — the querent's actions in laying the cards down carry interpretive meaning.

## Spreading on a Shape

Instead of a fixed layout, spreads can be created from geometrical or symbolic shapes.

### Five-pointed star spread:
Five cards at the five points of a star, oriented with one tip at the top (crown):
- **Head (top point):** The querent's thoughts
- **Left hand:** Internal influences (what is known to self but not others)
- **Right hand:** External environment (what is known to others but not self)
- **Left foot:** Internal factors the querent stands on
- **Right foot:** External factors the querent stands on
- **Center (sixth card):** What is in the person's heart

### Physical object shape spread (house):
Cards are laid in the shape of a house; each card is interpreted according to the function of the corresponding house part:
- Roof: What protects me
- Walls: What separates me from the surroundings
- Windows: The way I look at reality
- Door: What I let come into my life
- Chimney: What I let out

### World card spread:
Four cards at the corners of a rectangle (the four living creatures' positions), plus a fifth card at the center (the dancing figure):
- Bull (lower left): Body / material domain
- Lion (lower right): Desire / creative domain
- Human (upper left): Emotional domain
- Eagle (upper right): Intellectual domain
- Center: The querent's unified being acting in all four domains

### Wheel of Fortune spread:
Three cards for the three animals on the wheel (ascending, at peak, descending), a fourth card at the wheel's center (the driving factor), and optionally a fifth card below (the base / unchangeable external conditions).

### Lover card spread:
Three cards for the three standing figures (the querent as they are, the past they are disengaging from, the future they are advancing toward), with a fourth card above representing the angel's hint or divine direction.

### Magician spread (Johari window):
Based on the psychological Johari window model:
- Top card (Magician's face): The querent's basic feeling of self-identity
- Center (on the table): What is known both to self and others
- Left (outside the card frame, where the Magician looks): What is known to self but not to others
- Right: What is unknown to both self and others
- Bottom (below the table, which the Magician cannot see): What is known to others but not to self

## The Words Spread

### Purpose:
For focused questions that can be formulated as a short, clear sentence.

### Method:
1. The querent formulates a clear sentence for their question
2. After shuffling, lay one card for each word in the sentence (prepositions and connecting words join the following word; groups of words can share one card)
3. Lay the sentence cards in a horizontal row, left to right
4. Place one additional card below the row as the "final answer card"

### Position meanings:
- **Opening interrogative word (what, when, why):** The querent's status and attitude toward the query
- **Each subsequent word:** Interpreted according to the word's meaning and function in the sentence
- **Final answer card (below):** Summarizes the complete spread and represents a final answer to the question

### Example ("What bothers me in my relationship with Mira?"):
- "What" → querent's attitude and position in asking
- "Bothers" → the bothering factors from the querent's perspective
- "Me" → the querent's own role in the relationship
- "In my relationship" → the relationship itself, what it involves
- "With Mira" → the partner's position and role
- Final answer card → overall view and possible remedy

## Interpretation Guidelines and Principles

### On the symbolic language of directions:
- **Left (in the card frame):** The past; retraction; drawing inward
- **Right (in the card frame):** The future; expansion; moving outward
- **Top of the card:** Higher functions (reason, spiritual); the sky; celestial or divine influence
- **Bottom of the card:** Lower functions (desire, body); the earth; material reality; the abyss (when black) = subconscious depths, past trauma
- **Ascending diagonal (lower-left to upper-right):** Positive development and improvement over time
- **Descending diagonal (upper-left to lower-right):** Recession and decline over time

### On the right and left sides of the human body (as shown in cards):
Marseille figures are depicted as if facing us (like a real person in front of us, not a mirror):
- **Figure's right hand** = appears on the **card's left side** = action, initiative, hardness
- **Figure's left hand** = appears on the **card's right side** = reception, containment, softness

### On male and female figures:
- A male figure in a card can represent a woman acting in a traditionally masculine way, and vice versa
- Young figures: beginnings, first steps, naivety, stamina, rashness
- Older figures: maturity, experience, moderation

### On body parts of tarot figures:
- **Legs** = what we stand on; the material and physical domain; stability or movement
- **Pelvis** = desire and creative forces; sexual energy
- **Chest/heart area** = emotional receptivity; touching the chest indicates warmth and trust
- **Head** = the intellect; direction of thoughts; covered head = controlled thinking; loose hair = fresh and open thinking

### On colors:
- **White:** Pure spirituality, openness, sky, purity, innocence; can also mean frigidity or lack of life energy
- **Black:** Defined limits; shaded layers of the soul; the subconscious; dark or painful past; in alchemy: the starting point for refinement
- **Yellow:** Intelligence applied to practical needs; gold; material success; beneficial force; the earth (when the ground appears yellow = blessed conditions for growth)
- **Red:** Activity and movement; passion and energy; anger and aggression; fire; Mars/combativeness
- **Blue:** Attraction and inbound movement; deep feelings; empathy and compassion; water
- **Light blue:** Clarity, transparency, truth; a wider perspective; air
- **Green:** Growth, nature, fertility, new beginnings; the vegetable element
- **Flesh color:** What is alive and human; the body, sensuality, animal drives; openness, vulnerability

### On numbers appearing in cards:
- **1 (One):** Beginning, individuality, wholeness, the Magician, a new point of focus
- **2 (Two):** Opposition, duality, partnership or conflict, potential tension not yet released
- **3 (Three):** Dynamism, flow, fertility, creation; the triangle shape; sorcery
- **4 (Four):** Solid, stable, material, practical; the square; the four elements and four directions
- **5 (Five):** Disruption of stability; opening a new dimension; the five-pointed star (upright = benign, inverted = dark)
- **6 (Six):** Harmony, union of opposites (2×3); the Lover; the Star of David; marriage
- **7 (Seven):** Inner tension between abundance and instability; the rainbow; the seven-day week; the Chariot
- **8 (Eight):** Rational constructions, discernment, long patient work; Justice
- **9 (Nine):** Complexity, multiple possibilities, a strive toward perfection; the Hermit; mysticism
- **10 (Ten):** Totality, completion, opening a new cycle; the Wheel; the Sefirot

### Session dynamics and ethical guidelines:
- Do not be hasty to give a definitive answer immediately
- Describe the general impression first; present interpretations gradually and tentatively ("I think that…" or "I ask myself whether it's not…")
- Adapt what is said to what the querent can hear and accept at each moment
- Never present things in an absolute, rigid form: "this is the way things are," "such-and-such will happen"
- If the querent refuses a message, do not insist; return to the cards and try another direction
- Difficult content should come before the last quarter of the session; end on an uplifting, empowering tone
- The criterion for a successful reading is not immediate satisfaction but whether the querent considers it helpful in retrospect

## Using the Celtic Cross / Large Spreads

The author notes that in the open reading approach, **large, complicated spreads are generally not necessary** because the Marseille illustrations are rich enough in themselves. Too many cards can distract attention from difficult cards and create temptation to ignore them.

For readers who wish to use pre-defined layouts (including the Celtic Cross found in many tarot books), the open reading approach may still be applied: look at the whole picture formed by all positions before interpreting individual cards, and allow the function of each position to emerge from the actual cards rather than adhering rigidly to pre-assigned meanings.

When using any spread with fixed position meanings, this book recommends:
- Begin by scanning the entire spread as a visual whole
- Note what strikes you before applying positional labels
- Let strong visual impressions override or inform the positional meaning

## Quick Reference: Marseille-Specific Reading Techniques

### Working with the major suit alone:
Many French tarot readers — including Jodorowsky — use only the 22 major suit cards throughout their entire career. Three major cards from the Marseille deck are considered sufficient for a deep and productive reading.

### Fan technique (22 majors only):
Spread the 22 major cards facedown in a fan. The querent picks cards by the intuitive feel of their hand (no deliberate choice since they cannot see the faces). This satisfies both shuffling principles: querent acts, but choice is not consciously controlled.

### Using court cards as a projection tool:
Before the main reading, separate the court cards and spread them face up. Ask the querent to pick the one whose figure echoes their feelings by posture or facial expression. Ask them to describe what catches their attention and why they chose it. This helps querents who find it difficult to open up. Then proceed with a three-card major suit spread.

### Reading after the reading:
Cards left on a board after a consultation can be observed later for personal insights. Images that return to mind after a reading often carry the most important messages.

### Telephone and remote readings:
The reader shuffles while the querent on the phone says when to stop; the top card at that moment is drawn. This is accepted as a legitimate method consistent with the two shuffling principles.
