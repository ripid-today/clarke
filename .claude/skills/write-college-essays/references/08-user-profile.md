### MoMo — Context Paragraph

> At MoMo, Vietnam's leading e-wallet platform, I led the Bank Integrations team responsible for building and sustaining the infrastructure that allows millions of users to link their bank accounts directly to the MoMo app. The core challenge was multi-layered: on the technical side, each bank maintained its own API specifications, authentication standards, and system availability windows, which meant every new integration required building from a different baseline. To navigate this, I owned the full integration lifecycle — from drafting bank partnership contracts and interpreting State Bank circulars, to coordinating across legal, risk, product, design, and engineering teams to ship features that satisfied regulators without degrading user experience. I built project checklists and delivery timelines that became the team's standard operating procedure for every new bank onboarding. The result: the team achieved its annual OKRs in both transaction success rate and account linking success rate, metrics that directly determine MoMo's viability as a payment platform at scale.

### Ripid — Context Paragraph

Use verbatim or adapt as needed:

> In parallel with my work at MoMo, I co-founded Ripid, a social habit-tracking application built on the belief that sustained personal change is more durable when it happens inside a community rather than in isolation. The problem we set out to solve was straightforward: most habit-tracking apps optimize for individual streaks, which creates fragile motivation that breaks the moment life intervenes. Ripid replaced the streak with the group — users joined shared habit circles, checked in together, and held each other accountable in the way that a running partner or a study group does. At our peak, Ripid had 100,000 installs and 2,000 daily active users; within those numbers were people who completed their first book, maintained their first consistent morning routine, and found accountability partners across cities. My role spanned product strategy, user research, growth experimentation, and the day-to-day decisions of a two-sided community platform. What the experience ultimately revealed was a gap I had not anticipated: I could diagnose what the product needed to grow, but I could not construct the financial model or the business architecture that would let it grow sustainably. Every major decision — whether to raise, when to pivot, how to price — required a vocabulary and a rigor I did not yet possess. Ripid taught me the difference between operating a product and leading a business.
