# 06 — Prompt Decoding: How to Decode Any Admission Prompt

## Three Layers of Every Prompt

Every admission prompt has three layers. Answering only the first layer produces a mediocre essay. The best essays answer all three simultaneously.

**Layer 1 — The Surface Question:** What are they literally asking? Identify the subject and the form (describe, explain, tell us about, discuss).

**Layer 2 — The Deeper Question:** What do they actually want to know about you? The surface question is a vehicle. What specific information about the applicant is being sought?

**Layer 3 — The Risk They Are Addressing:** What failure mode in candidates is this prompt designed to screen for? Every prompt is an instrument of risk management. Understanding the risk unlocks the real answer.

---

## Applying the Three Layers to Common Prompts

### "Why this program?" / "Why NTU IMBA?"

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| Surface | Your reasons for choosing this program |
| Deeper | Do you know what you actually need, and have you thought specifically about whether this program provides it? |
| Risk | Mass-applying candidates who haven't researched; applicants with generic goals that any program would serve |

**Implication:** Listing program features is not an answer. Showing why *those specific features* address *your specific gap* — diagnosed precisely — is an answer.

---

### "Why now?"

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| Surface | Your reasoning for applying at this stage of your career |
| Deeper | Is this a deliberate, well-timed decision, or are you escaping something? |
| Risk | Candidates who are running away from a current situation rather than moving toward a specific goal; candidates who would have applied at any point without clear motivation |

**Implication:** The "why now" answer must come from a specific, recent development — not a general accumulation. For this user: the intersection of the MoMo Bank Integrations depth + Ripid's revealed strategic gap creates an answer that is both specific and honest.

---

### "Describe a leadership experience"

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| Surface | Tell a story about leading |
| Deeper | How do you handle complexity, uncertainty, and other people? What is your model of leadership? |
| Risk | Candidates who describe a team achievement without individual accountability; candidates who confuse "being part of a successful team" with "leading" |

**Implication:** The essay must show personal judgment and personal accountability — not team success attributed to collective effort. The specific decision you made, the specific uncertainty you navigated, the specific cost you absorbed.

---

### "Tell us about a community or entrepreneurial experience"

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| Surface | Your involvement in a community or a venture |
| Deeper | Do you give back? Do you think beyond yourself? Do you understand impact beyond immediate metrics? |
| Risk | Purely self-interested candidates; scholarship candidates who cannot articulate impact beyond their own gain |

**Implication:** The Ripid story, for this user, answers this at the community level (2,000 people using the app to complete their first book, build a morning routine, find accountability partners) and at the entrepreneurial level (product strategy, growth decisions, the strategic gap revealed). Both layers must be present.

## The Prompt Decoding Process (Step-by-Step)

Apply this process before drafting any essay:

1. **Underline the active verb** in the prompt (describe, explain, tell, discuss, reflect)
2. **Circle the subject noun** (leadership, community, goals, challenge, achievement)
3. **Ask:** What is the implicit fear behind this prompt? What applicant type is being screened out?
4. **Ask:** What specific evidence from my life most directly addresses that fear?
5. **Ask:** What is the single most important thing the reader should know about me from this essay — if they remember only one thing?
6. **Draft a one-sentence thesis:** "This essay shows that I [specific claim] because [specific evidence]."

The thesis sentence does not appear in the essay. It is the compass that ensures every paragraph points the same direction.

## Common Prompt Traps

**Answering the question you wished they asked.** The most common failure. The writer has a story they want to tell and finds a way to tell it regardless of what the prompt asks. The reader notices. Always return to the prompt's active verb.

**Spending most words on context.** Context serves the answer; it is not the answer. If more than 30% of the essay is setup, the actual answer is being crowded out.

**Treating multi-part prompts as requiring equal distribution.** A prompt asking "describe a challenge and what you learned from it" allocates most of its value to "what you learned" — the challenge itself is only the setup. Writers who spend 80% on the challenge and 20% on the learning have inverted the proportions.

**For "why us" prompts — the feature-listing trap.** "NTU IMBA offers a strong network and proximity to the Singapore financial market" is a feature list. "The NTU IMBA's finance curriculum, delivered within Asia's most liquid cross-border banking market, addresses the specific gap I diagnosed in my ability to construct institutional strategy — a gap that three years of payment infrastructure work clarified but could not close" is an answer.

## Prompt Decoding Applied: NTU Essay 1

**Surface prompt:** Why this program / Why now / 3–5 year plan (400–500 words)

| Layer | This User's Answer |
|---|---|
| Surface | Why NTU IMBA, why at this career stage, what the next 3–5 years look like |
| Deeper | Can this applicant articulate a precise, credible gap — not a general aspiration — that this program specifically addresses? |
| Risk | Applicants with vague goals ("I want to grow my career") or generic program selection ("NTU is a great school") |

**One-sentence thesis:** "This essay shows that I have diagnosed a specific gap between product execution and institutional strategy — diagnosed through three years of bank integration work at MoMo and the strategic ceiling I encountered at Ripid — and that the NTU IMBA is the precise instrument for closing that gap, not a general career upgrade."

**Structural implication:** Essay 1 should open in a moment that embodies the gap (not a career goals statement), build to the diagnosis, and land on the program choice as a logical conclusion, not an arbitrary preference.
