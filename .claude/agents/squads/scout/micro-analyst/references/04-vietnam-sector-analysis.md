# Vietnam Sector Analysis — Key Metrics and Investment Frameworks

## 1. Banking Sector

### Why Banking Is Different
Banks require a completely different analytical framework from industrial companies. Key distinction: banks are levered financial intermediaries — a bank's "inventory" is money, and its "product" is the spread between borrowing and lending rates. Standard industrial metrics (P/E, D/E, FCF) either don't apply or require significant interpretation.

### Key Banking Metrics

**NIM (Net Interest Margin)**
- Definition: (Interest Income − Interest Expense) ÷ Average Interest-Earning Assets
- Target: >3.5% = healthy; 3.0-3.5% = adequate; <2.5% = margin under pressure
- Vietnam context: NIM compressed in 2023-2024 as SBV pushed banks to reduce lending rates to support growth → banks accepted lower margins to maintain credit targets
- NIM expansion drivers: rising lending rates, falling deposit rates, CASA ratio improvement, shift to higher-yield retail loans

**NPL Ratio (Non-Performing Loan)**
- Definition: NPL = loans overdue >90 days ÷ total loans
- Healthy: <2% = normal credit cycle losses
- Watch: 2.0-3.0% = elevated; banks may need to increase provisioning → profit impact
- Stress: >3.0% = systemic issue in loan book; provisioning costs will hit earnings significantly
- **Vietnam-specific warning:** Vietnamese banks regularly reschedule troubled loans (circular 02/2023 COVID rescheduling; circular 06/2023 real estate) — rescheduled loans are technically "current" but may be non-performing in substance. Adjusted NPL (adding rescheduled loans) can be 2-3× reported NPL for some banks exposed to developer loans.
- Provisioning expense: watch loan loss provision/average loans ratio; rising = bank is acknowledging credit quality issues

**CAR (Capital Adequacy Ratio)**
- Definition: Tier 1 + Tier 2 Capital ÷ Risk-Weighted Assets (Basel II formula)
- Minimum regulatory: 8% (Basel II; SBV standard)
- Comfortable buffer: 9-10%
- Capital-raising risk: CAR < 9% = bank may need to issue new shares or subordinated debt → dilutive for existing shareholders
- Vietnam banks lag global peers in capital adequacy; several VN30 banks operate at 9-10% CAR vs. 12-15% for Singapore/Thailand banks

**CASA Ratio (Current Account + Savings Account)**
- Definition: (Current accounts + Savings accounts) ÷ Total deposits
- Why it matters: CASA deposits are zero-cost or near-zero-cost (checking accounts) or low-cost (savings accounts) → higher CASA = lower funding cost = higher NIM
- Strong: >35% CASA = structurally efficient funding
- Typical Vietnam range: 20-40% (Techcombank historically 45-50% = standout)
- TCB's high CASA advantage: driven by ecosystem strategy (partnerships with VHM for home buyer accounts, salary account relationships)

**Loan-to-Deposit Ratio (LDR)**
- Definition: Total Loans ÷ Total Deposits
- Regulatory ceiling: SBV typically sets at 85-90%
- Aggressive lending: >80% = bank is close to maximum; limited room to grow loans faster than deposits
- Conservative: <65% = significant room to grow loans; but may indicate weak demand or credit selectivity

**Foreign Ownership Limit (FOL) — The Foreign Investor Bottleneck**
- Vietnamese banks: 30% FOL — hard cap; no exceptions without special government approval
- Most major banks (VCB, TCB, BID) are already at or near 30% FOL
- Impact: foreign investors cannot increase Vietnam bank holdings above 30%; creates supply constraint for foreign demand → premium price for foreign investors willing to pay FOL-limited stock
- Strategic partnerships: some banks have issued shares to foreign strategic investors (VCB to Mizuho at 15%; TCB to HSBC historically) — reduces free float available for FOL purchases

### Vietnam Banking Investment Framework

**Growth vs. Value in Banking:**
- **Growth banks:** TCB (Techcombank), MBB (MB Bank), VPB (VPBanks) — focused on retail/consumer credit; higher ROE, higher NPL risk, growth-oriented management
- **Value banks:** VCB (Vietcombank) — state-owned, pristine loan book, conservative management; trades at premium P/B for quality; income-oriented investors
- **Turnaround candidates:** STB (Sacombank) — recovering from 2016-2022 NPL workout; still discounted; upside if bad debt resolution completes

## 2. Real Estate Sector

### VAS Revenue Recognition Warning
Real estate is the sector most distorted by VAS accounting. Never use VAS-reported net income for a real estate company without understanding revenue recognition timing.

**VAS approach:** Revenue booked when buyer signs purchase agreement and makes 20-30% deposit
**IFRS approach:** Revenue booked at unit delivery (2-3 years after signing)

**Consequence:** VAS real estate earnings look:
- Massively profitable in years with heavy pre-sales signing
- Nearly unprofitable in years with heavy delivery but low new signing
- **Correction:** Use pre-sales value (contract value signed, not revenue recognized) as the leading indicator of future earnings; P/pre-sales is more useful than P/E for developer valuation

### Key Real Estate Metrics

**Pre-Sales Value**
- Definition: total VND value of purchase contracts signed in the period (not revenue)
- Leading indicator: pre-sales signed today → recognized in VAS revenue 1-3 years later → earnings follow
- Strong: pre-sales growing >20% year-over-year = future earnings growth locked in
- Weak: pre-sales declining for 2+ quarters = earnings headwinds in 1-2 years

**Land Bank Value vs. Market Cap**
- Land bank at cost = total land use right book value (heavily understated vs. market value)
- Land bank at market = developer's estimate (often optimistic)
- Method: land bank at market ÷ market cap; if > 1.0 = land value alone exceeds market cap = deep value hypothesis
- **Caution:** Assumes land can be converted to sellable units; zoning changes, legal disputes, and capital constraints can prevent this

**Debt/Equity for Real Estate**
- Higher leverage is normal in real estate (asset-backed); but Vietnam RE crisis 2022-23 demonstrated limits
- D/E > 2.0 = elevated risk for Vietnam real estate; especially dangerous if debt is short-term and assets are long-dated
- D/E > 3.0 = avoid unless asset quality is exceptional and refinancing risk is managed

**Inventory (Unsold Units) in Months of Sales**
- Definition: (units in inventory ÷ monthly sales pace)
- >24 months = serious demand problem; forces discounting → margin erosion
- 12-24 months = elevated; watch for improvement
- <12 months = healthy; demand absorbing supply

### Vietnam RE Cycle Context
- 2018-2021: boom — VHM IPO, wave of new developments, credit-fueled buying
- 2022-2023: crisis — high interest rates, bond market freeze (corporate bond defaults), developer credit tightened; VHM, NVL, PDR fell 50-80%
- 2024 onwards: recovery — interest rates fell, credit eased, government pushed bond market cleanup; developers slowly recovering
- **Investment timing:** RE cycle recovery is structural (2-4 years); best entry is when developers' pre-sales begin recovering and balance sheet risk is stabilizing

## 3. Manufacturing and Industrials

### Steel — HPG Focus
HPG (Hoa Phat Group) is Vietnam's dominant steel company and a key industrial bellwether.

**Capacity (2024):** ~8.5 million tons/year crude steel capacity (Dung Quat integrated complex + Hai Duong)
**Product mix:** HRC (hot-rolled coil, Dung Quat focus), construction rebar, wire rod

**Key metrics:**
- EBITDA per tonne: Vietnam domestic steel margin $40-90/tonne historically; below $30/tonne = pain; above $100/tonne = boom
- Input cost per tonne: coking coal (~60% of input cost) + iron ore (~20%); track both
- Domestic demand split: construction 60%, manufacturing 25%, export 15%
- Export capability: when domestic oversupplied, HPG exports to ASEAN; export margin typically lower than domestic

**HPG investment framework:**
- Bullish case: credit growth accelerates → construction demand picks up → HPG volumes rise + margins recover from cycle low
- Bearish case: Chinese steel dumping intensifies → Vietnam domestic prices compress below HPG's cost curve → losses per tonne
- Neutral environment: HPG operates at 60-70% utilization; break-even margin; waiting for cycle turn

**Capacity Utilization**
- >80% = pricing power; factory economics favorable; margins above average
- 60-80% = normal range; margins moderate
- <60% = overcapacity; margin pressure across sector; weaker players face losses

### Manufacturing / Light Industry
- **Order backlog in months:** rising backlog = demand strength; backlog declining = demand softening (leading indicator)
- **Export order growth:** track via GSO industrial production data and company quarterly reports
- **Energy cost monitoring:** Vietnamese manufacturers are energy-intensive (electricity, fuel); power tariff increases directly compress margins; industrial electricity prices have risen in 2022-2023

## 4. Consumer Sector

### SSSG (Same-Store-Sales Growth)
- Most critical metric for retail chains (MWG Mobile World, PNJ Phu Nhuan Jewelry, Bách Hoá Xanh groceries)
- **Definition:** Revenue growth at stores open for > 12 months (excludes new store openings)
- **Why it matters:** Total revenue can grow from new store openings even as existing stores decline → SSSG strips this out and shows organic health
- Positive SSSG = market share gains or category growth; consumers spending more per transaction or more frequently
- Negative SSSG for 2+ quarters = saturation, competition taking share, or macro consumer weakness

**MWG example:** When SSSG turned negative in 2022-2023, it signaled consumer electronics saturation in Vietnam ahead of broader macro data — early warning indicator.

### Gross Margin Trend
- Stable or expanding gross margin = pricing power + supply chain efficiency
- Compressing gross margin = competition on price, input cost inflation, or mix shift to lower-margin products
- Check against industry: VNM vs. Moc Chau Milk (comparable product), PNJ vs. unbranded jewelry vendors (premium brand sustainable?)

### Inventory Turnover
- Consumer goods target: >4× per year (inventory turns over 4 times in 12 months)
- Electronics target: >6× (electronics are short product-cycle goods; sitting inventory = value erosion)
- Low inventory turnover + revenue growth = strong demand pulling through
- Low inventory turnover + flat revenue = demand problem; potential write-down

### Distribution Network Coverage
- Vietnam consumer companies need presence in three major metro clusters to claim national coverage:
  1. Ho Chi Minh City and surrounding Binh Duong, Dong Nai, Long An (Southern Vietnam; largest population center)
  2. Hanoi and surrounding Hung Yen, Bac Ninh (Northern Vietnam; second population center)
  3. Da Nang and Central Vietnam corridor (bridge between North and South; growing middle class)
- Companies with all three = national brand; pricing power premium
- Companies with only HCMC + HN = regional; growth story if expanding; risk if competitors fill the gap first

## 5. Foreign Ownership Limits by Sector

FOL caps directly affect stock liquidity and price premium.

| Sector | FOL Cap | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Banking | 30% | Hard cap; strategic investor allocation carves out separately |
| Securities/Finance | 49% | Standard commercial cap |
| Most commercial sectors | 49% | Standard |
| Real estate (residential) | 49% general; restrictions on land use | Foreign ownership of apartments limited; companies owning land face additional review |
| Aviation / Airports | Case-by-case; typically <35% | National security sensitivity |
| Telecommunications | Case-by-case; typically <49% | Strategic sector |
| Media / Publishing | Typically 0% or restricted | Content control |
| Energy / Oil & Gas | Case-by-case; SOE dominant | PVN subsidiaries have varying FOL |

**FOL premium mechanics:**
- When a stock approaches FOL ceiling: foreign demand exists but supply of shares available to foreigners is exhausted → foreigners pay above domestic price on HOSE for the remaining shares (FOL premium typically 2-10%)
- When FOL is raised: immediate re-rating as new foreign buyers can enter → price jumps, then normalizes

**Investment opportunity from FOL mechanics:**
- Companies with FOL room (foreign currently at 20% vs. 49% cap) = foreign buying headroom; if stock becomes attractive to foreign funds, demand can push price up as foreigners enter
- Companies at FOL (foreign at 49%) = no additional foreign buying; stock trades at domestic valuation only; monitor for FOL raise announcements which create positive catalysts
