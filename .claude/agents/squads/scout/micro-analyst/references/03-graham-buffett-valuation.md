# Graham-Buffett Valuation Frameworks for Vietnam

## 1. Graham's Defensive Investor Criteria

From "The Intelligent Investor" (Benjamin Graham, revised edition), Chapter 14: Stock Selection for the Defensive Investor. These criteria were designed to identify undervalued companies with sufficient safety margin for conservative investors.

### The Seven Criteria (Original)
1. **Adequate size:** Minimum size to ensure some staying power and operational stability
2. **Sufficiently strong financial condition:** Current ratio > 2.0; long-term debt ≤ net current assets
3. **Earnings stability:** Positive earnings in each of the past 10 years
4. **Dividend record:** Uninterrupted dividends for at least 20 years
5. **Earnings growth:** EPS at least 33% higher over past 10 years (3% annual growth floor)
6. **Moderate P/E ratio:** P/E ≤ 15× average 3-year earnings
7. **Moderate price-to-assets:** P/B ≤ 1.5×; combined P/E × P/B ≤ 22.5

### Vietnam Adaptation (5-Year Minimum)
The 10-year history requirement eliminates most Vietnam mid-cap companies (many listed post-2010). Adjust for Vietnam context:

| Original Criterion | Vietnam Adaptation | Rationale |
|-------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| 10-year earnings history | 5-year earnings history minimum | Vietnam market depth; many quality companies listed < 10 years ago |
| 20-year dividend record | 5-year uninterrupted dividend | Same rationale; focus on recent stability |
| EPS growth 33% over 10 years | EPS growth 20% over 5 years | Equivalent nominal trajectory in Vietnam |
| Current ratio > 2.0 | Current ratio > 1.5 | Vietnam banks provide backup liquidity; strict 2× eliminates most candidates |
| P/E ≤ 15× | P/E ≤ 15× trailing | Same — Vietnam index trades at 12-16× |
| P/B ≤ 1.5× | P/B ≤ 2.0× | Vietnamese land appreciation inflates book value; adjust threshold up |

**Graham's combined formula (still applicable):** P/E × P/B ≤ 22.5 — this is the most powerful single screen; at P/E of 12 and P/B of 1.5 (18.0), the company passes. At P/E of 15 and P/B of 2.0 (30.0), it fails.

### Vietnam Companies That Could Meet Adapted Graham Criteria
These sectors are most likely to produce defensive Graham candidates:
- **State-owned banks (post-recapitalization):** Strong balance sheets, consistent earnings, dividends; but low ROE limits P/B premium
- **Vinamilk (VNM):** Long dividend history, stable earnings, defensible business; watch P/E
- **Established utilities:** REE, GAS — stable earnings, dividends, reasonable balance sheets
- **Large-cap industrials at cycle troughs:** HPG, HPG at steel cycle lows; may meet criteria temporarily

## 2. Graham's Net-Net Analysis

### NCAV Calculation
Net Current Asset Value (NCAV) = Total Current Assets − Total Liabilities (not just current liabilities; all liabilities)

**Graham's Rule:** Buy stocks trading below 67% of NCAV (the "two-thirds rule")

**Rationale:** Current assets (cash, receivables, inventory) minus ALL liabilities = the minimum liquidation value of the business. Buying below two-thirds of this provides a margin of safety even in worst-case liquidation scenarios.

### Vietnam Net-Net Candidates
Vietnam stocks trading below NCAV are rare but can appear in:
- **SOE partial privatizations:** SOEs converted to joint-stock companies with large inherited asset bases (inventory, receivables, land) relative to small publicly-traded market caps
- **Distressed companies:** Companies in temporary financial difficulty but with recoverable assets
- **Niche industrial companies:** Small manufacturers with large raw material inventories and receivables trading at distressed P/B

### VAS Adjustment for Net-Net
When calculating NCAV from VAS accounts:
1. Use total current assets (from balance sheet)
2. Subtract ALL liabilities: current liabilities + long-term liabilities + off-balance-sheet guarantees (if material)
3. Apply haircut to receivables: if receivables days > 180, apply 30% haircut (questionable collectability)
4. Apply haircut to inventory: if inventory days > 180, apply 20% haircut (forced sale discount)
5. Adjusted NCAV = (adjusted current assets) − total liabilities

### Patience Warning for Vietnam Net-Nets
Graham identified that net-nets often remain undervalued for years without a catalyst to unlock value. In Vietnam:
- Controlling shareholders may not have interest in shareholder value maximization
- Corporate governance mechanisms to force value release (activist investors, proxy fights) are weak in Vietnam
- **Clarke's approach:** Require a specific catalyst when investing in net-nets (SOE restructuring announcement, privatization, management change, sector recovery timeline); without catalyst, the discount can persist indefinitely

## 3. Buffett's Economic Moat Framework

From Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway annual letters (particularly 1986, 1993, 1999) and applied by Charlie Munger in numerous speeches. The core question: "What prevents competitors from taking this company's customers and profits?"

### The Five Moat Sources (with Vietnam Examples)

**1. Cost Advantage**
- Definition: ability to produce at lower cost per unit than all competitors at similar scale
- Sources: economies of scale (fixed costs spread over more units), proprietary process, advantaged raw material access, geography
- Vietnam examples:
  - **HPG (Hoa Phat Group):** Vertical integration from coking coal (own mines in Australia considered) to finished steel; largest blast furnace capacity in Vietnam → scale cost advantage over smaller competitors; exports when domestic oversupplied
  - **Masan Consumer (MSN via Masan):** nationwide distribution network for packaged food → lower distribution cost per unit than competitors; cost advantage reinforces market share
  - **PetroVietnam Gas (GAS):** Pipeline infrastructure monopoly → essentially zero-cost transportation once infrastructure built; new entrant cost to replicate = billions of USD

**2. Switching Costs**
- Definition: customers incur significant cost, risk, or inconvenience when switching to a competitor
- Sources: data integration, workflow dependency, relationship investment, multi-year contracts
- Vietnam examples:
  - **Techcombank (TCB):** Large corporate relationships with developer clients (VHM, MBBank ecosystem); embedded in payroll systems; switching bank for corporate treasury = 6-12 months of work
  - **FPT Software:** Enterprise IT clients (banking system implementations, ERP) have 5-10 year switching cost horizons once FPT's code is running core operations
  - **Healthcare IT (rare but emerging):** Hospital management systems; clinics don't switch software mid-operation

**3. Intangible Assets (Brands and Patents)**
- Definition: consumers pay more for the brand; or patents prevent competitors from copying the product
- Vietnam reality: brand moats exist but are shallow vs. global competitors; Vietnamese brands can be displaced by Chinese or Korean alternatives faster than Western precedents suggest
- Vietnam examples:
  - **VNM (Vinamilk):** Brand trust in dairy; mothers won't switch to unknown brand for infant formula; but trust was damaged by 2008 melamine scandal (Chinese milk); recovered, but shows brand fragility
  - **Masan Consumer:** Chinsu fish sauce, Kokomi, Omachi brands; significant switching costs in taste-sensitive packaged food; brand is pricing power
  - **PNJ (Phu Nhuan Jewelry):** Jewelry brand with quality certification; trust premium over unbranded jewelry vendors

**4. Network Effects**
- Definition: the service becomes more valuable to each user as more users join
- Vietnam reality: network effect businesses are largely not listed on Vietnam exchanges (VNPay, MoMo not yet listed)
- Nearest listed examples:
  - **VNM in school milk distribution:** Once VNM is the school milk supplier, schools, parents, and students are part of the distribution network; each new school adds value to distribution density
  - **FPT Telecom:** Broadband internet has mild network effects in dense urban buildings (building-level infrastructure gives FPT competitive advantage over point-by-point competitor entry)
  - **STB (Sacombank) ATM/banking network:** More ATMs → more customers → more merchants accept Sacombank → more customers. Classic banking network effect, though weaker now with interbank ATM sharing.

**5. Efficient Scale**
- Definition: the market is only large enough for one or a few efficient operators; new entrants cannot justify investment
- Vietnam examples:
  - **GAS (Petrovietnam Gas):** Gas pipeline infrastructure in southern Vietnam; market size doesn't justify a second pipeline; monopoly by efficient scale
  - **Airports (ACV — Airports Corporation of Vietnam):** State-controlled but relevant as listed entity; airport monopolies in each city = efficient scale moat; no economics for competing airport in same metro
  - **Port operators (GMD — Gemadept, VSC — Vietnam Container Shipping):** Major deepwater ports are efficient scale; building competing deepwater port at same location is economically nonsensical

### Moat Durability Assessment — Vietnam Adjustment
Most Vietnam moats are **narrower and shallower** than equivalent Western companies:
- Regulatory protection can be removed by government (creates moats and destroys them)
- Brand loyalty erodes faster in a rapidly changing consumer society (first-generation middle class with no legacy brand attachment)
- Chinese competition intensifies regularly (low-cost manufacturing → low-cost end products enter Vietnam market)
- **Clarke rule:** Require higher ROE threshold (>18%) to claim durable moat; accept lower threshold only with explicit competitive advantage documented

## 4. Quality Company Screen

### Five Quality Criteria (All Must Be Met for "Quality" Label)

**Criterion 1: ROE > 15% sustained for 5+ years**
- Not one year spikes; average ROE over 5 years must exceed 15%
- A single high-ROE year from leverage or asset sale doesn't qualify
- Check Dupont decomposition: is ROE from margin, turnover, or leverage?
  - Margin-driven ROE (net margin expansion) = highest quality
  - Turnover-driven ROE (revenue relative to assets rising) = solid
  - Leverage-driven ROE (debt-to-equity rising) = NOT quality

**Criterion 2: Owner-Operator with Significant Equity Stake**
- Founding family or management personally holds >15% of shares (aligns interests with shareholders)
- Ideally founder still involved in strategy (not just a financial investor holding shares)
- Vietnam examples: HPG (Tran Dinh Long, founder, >20% stake), Masan (Nguyen Dang Quang, founder)
- **Warning:** SOEs are controlled by state, not owner-operators; governance is different; require additional margin of safety

**Criterion 3: Low Dilution History**
- Shares outstanding flat or declining over 5 years
- No serial equity issuance without proportionate earnings growth
- Check: rights issues, public offerings, ESOP/ESOW warrant programs
- Vietnam concern: companies under financial stress issue shares to raise cash; this is value destruction per share
- **Acceptable exception:** Shares issued for clear, high-ROIC investment that will grow EPS proportionately; rare

**Criterion 4: FCF Conversion > 80%**
- FCF consistently > 80% of net income over 5 years
- Demonstrates actual cash generation, not accounting profits
- Calculation: FCF = OCF − maintenance CapEx (not total CapEx; growth CapEx is elective)
- If growth CapEx is large: acceptable to use OCF/Net Income as proxy

**Criterion 5: Dividend Discipline**
- Dividends growing or stable; no cuts in past 5 years
- Dividend payout sustainable relative to FCF (payout < FCF)
- Vietnam preference: cash dividends > stock dividends (stock dividends dilute; cash dividends return value)
