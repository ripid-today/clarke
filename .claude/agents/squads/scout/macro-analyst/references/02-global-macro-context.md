# Global Macro Context for Vietnam Investments

## 1. Federal Reserve Transmission to Vietnam

### The Core Mechanism
The Federal Reserve's interest rate decisions transmit to Vietnam via three channels: (1) USD strength vs. VND, (2) EM fund flows (risk-on / risk-off), and (3) global demand for manufactured goods. Understanding which channel dominates in a given cycle determines the investment response.

### Channel 1: USD Strength → VND Pressure → Equity Outflows
- Fed hikes → US interest rates rise → USD becomes more attractive to global investors → DXY (Dollar Index) appreciates
- DXY appreciation → EM currencies under pressure → VND depreciates (SBV manages this, but market pressure builds)
- VND depreciation risk → foreign investors in Vietnam equities face currency loss on top of equity loss → accelerate selling → VN-Index declines even if fundamentals are unchanged
- **Rule of thumb:** DXY above 105 = meaningful headwind for Vietnam equities; DXY above 110 = severe headwind historically correlated with 15-25% VN-Index declines

### Channel 2: EM Fund Flows — The Most Direct Channel
- Vietnam is classified as Frontier Market (MSCI) and Secondary Emerging Market (FTSE); Vietnam equities sit in EM/Frontier funds globally
- Fed hawkish pivot → global risk-off → EM fund managers reduce Vietnam exposure → selling pressure
- Fed dovish pivot / rate cuts → global risk-on → EM inflows → Vietnam equities re-rate upward
- **Timeline:** Fed action (announcement) → Vietnam market reaction: 2-4 weeks via EM fund flow rebalancing; the initial reaction is often faster (days) via futures and direct institutional selling

### Channel 3: Global Demand for Manufactured Goods
- Fed tightening → US consumer spending slows → import demand falls → Samsung's electronics revenues under pressure → Samsung's Vietnam factories reduce production orders
- This is a slower channel (6-12 months) but ultimately more fundamental — it hits Vietnam's actual GDP growth, not just market sentiment

### DXY as the Leading Indicator
| DXY Level | Vietnam Market Signal |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Below 98 | Favorable — EM tailwind; likely foreign buying into Vietnam |
| 98-103 | Neutral — manageable; SBV can defend VND without heavy intervention |
| 103-107 | Caution — foreign selling risk; VND depreciation pressure mounting |
| Above 107 | Adverse — significant headwind; historical correlation with VN-Index weakness |
| Above 110 | Severe — SBV faces reserve drain; macro stress environment |

### Fed Pivot Playbook for Vietnam
When Fed signals rate cuts:
1. DXY weakens (anticipatory) → VND appreciation pressure → SBV may buy USD to prevent excessive VND strength
2. EM funds start adding Vietnam exposure in anticipation → VN-Index re-rates
3. Foreign institutional buying confirms → VN30 large caps rally first (most liquid)
4. Typical lag from first cut to full EM re-rating: 3-6 months
5. Historical magnitude: 2019 Fed pivot → VN-Index +18% in 6 months; 2009 Fed QE → VN-Index recovery sustained

### Sources for Fed Tracking
- Federal Reserve FOMC statements: federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm
- Fed Dot Plot: released quarterly with projections; signals rate path
- CME FedWatch Tool: fedwatch.tool — real-time market-implied rate probabilities
- Federal Reserve Beige Book: regional economic conditions; useful for reading US demand

## 2. China Nexus

### Trade Relationship Scale
- China is Vietnam's largest trading partner (bilateral trade ~$175B in 2023)
- Vietnam imports from China: ~$110B — approximately 25% of Vietnam's total imports
- Vietnam exports to China: ~$60B — approximately 13% of Vietnam's total exports
- Net position: Vietnam runs a significant trade deficit with China (~$50B) — this is structural (Vietnam imports Chinese inputs to assemble and export to the West)

### China Imports to Vietnam: What Flows
The composition of Vietnam's China imports is critical for understanding manufacturing cost structure:
- Machinery and equipment: ~30% of imports from China — capital goods for manufacturing expansion
- Textiles and fabric: ~15% — raw materials for Vietnam's garment export industry
- Electronics components: ~15% — circuit boards, displays, components for Vietnamese assembly
- Chemicals and plastics: ~10% — industrial raw materials
- Iron and steel: ~7% — construction and manufacturing

**Investment implication:** China supply chain disruption (port congestion, factory closures, tariffs) hits Vietnam manufacturers within 4-8 weeks via input availability and price. Track Caixin PMI as proxy.

### China Slowdown Impact on Vietnam — Two Vectors
**Negative vector (supply chain disruption):**
- China economic slowdown → Chinese factories reduce production → fewer components available for Vietnamese manufacturers → Vietnam IIP declines → exports fall
- Particularly acute for electronics manufacturers dependent on Chinese component supply

**Negative vector (reduced export demand):**
- China direct consumer demand for Vietnamese products is not large, but:
- Chinese tourists to Vietnam (pre-COVID: ~5M/year) represent meaningful services export revenue
- China buying Vietnamese agricultural products (fruits, seafood) — disruption creates export surplus, price weakness domestically

**Positive vector (supply chain shift / China+1 strategy):**
- Companies reducing China concentration → looking for manufacturing alternatives → Vietnam is primary beneficiary
- Western multinationals' China+1 strategy has already driven FDI surge; this accelerates when China-US tensions rise
- This is structural, not cyclical — Vietnam benefits over a 5-10 year horizon from supply chain regionalization

**Tracking indicator:** Chinese manufacturing PMI (Caixin) as leading indicator for Vietnam's manufacturing order book — correlation runs ~6-8 weeks ahead.

### China PMI as Leading Indicator
| Caixin China PMI | Signal for Vietnam Manufacturing |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| Above 52 | Positive — Chinese supply chain active; components flowing; Vietnam order book healthy |
| 50-52 | Neutral — watch for direction |
| Below 50 | Caution — Chinese slowdown starting to reduce input supply; Vietnam IIP risk |
| Below 48 sustained | Warning — significant supply chain disruption risk; Vietnam exports likely to disappoint |

### China-Specific Risks to Monitor
1. **Property sector debt crisis:** Evergrande-style defaults reduce construction activity → less demand for Vietnamese steel (HPG exports to China occasionally), reduces Chinese consumer confidence
2. **Export subsidies and dumping:** China overcapacity → Chinese firms dump steel, solar panels, EVs at below-cost prices → competes with Vietnam's exports; HPG particularly at risk from Chinese steel dumping
3. **Renminbi depreciation:** CNY weakens → Chinese exports cheaper globally → Vietnamese exporters (textiles, electronics, footwear) face more competition → margin pressure
4. **Geopolitical tension:** US-China tech decoupling = Vietnam wins FDI; but military tension in South China Sea = severe risk-off for Vietnam equities

## 3. ASEAN and Trade Agreement Integration

### ASEAN Free Trade Area (AFTA / ATIGA)
- ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement: most goods at 0% tariff within the 10 ASEAN members
- Full implementation completed 2018; Vietnam benefits as both importer and exporter within ASEAN
- Practical implication: ASEAN companies can access Vietnam market competitively; Vietnam companies can export to Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines without tariffs
- Investment angle: companies serving the ASEAN market from a Vietnam base have a structural tariff advantage vs. non-ASEAN competitors

### CPTPP (Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership)
- Members: Australia, Brunei, Canada, Chile, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, Peru, Singapore, Vietnam
- Vietnam signed 2018, ratified 2019
- Key benefit: access to Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Mexico, Japan, Peru, Chile markets with preferential tariffs
- Particularly valuable for Vietnam textiles/garments (major tariff reductions to Canada, Japan, Australia)
- Rules of origin requirement: product must contain sufficient CPTPP-origin content to qualify for preferential tariffs (significant compliance detail; garment companies need to source fabric from CPTPP members to qualify)
- **Investment signal:** Vietnamese garment companies (TCM, TNG) that have qualified for CPTPP origin benefits have structural export cost advantage

### RCEP (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership)
- Members: ASEAN 10 + China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand
- Entered into force 2022
- Largest trade agreement by GDP covered (~30% of global GDP)
- Vietnam's position: RCEP creates supply chain integration across the region; Vietnam firms can source from China, Japan, Korea under RCEP rules of origin and then export finished goods with RCEP benefits
- Practical benefit: more flexible rules of origin than CPTPP — makes Vietnamese manufacturers' supply chains more RCEP-compliant
- **Investment signal:** manufacturing companies with pan-ASEAN supply chains benefit from RCEP operational efficiency

### EVFTA (EU-Vietnam Free Trade Agreement)
- Entered into force 2020
- Eliminates ~99% of tariffs over 10-year period
- Vietnam gains EU market access; EU gains Vietnam market access
- **Key sectors benefiting:** textiles (Vietnam exports €7B/year to EU), seafood (tariff elimination critical for shrimp/catfish), footwear, electronics
- Compliance requirement: strict EU origin rules and sustainability standards (labor rights, environmental) for EVFTA benefits — compliance cost but also quality signal for European buyers

### UKVFTA (UK-Vietnam Free Trade Agreement)
- Post-Brexit; entered into force 2021
- Mirrors much of EVFTA structure; maintains access to UK market post-EU exit

## 4. Commodity Impact on Vietnam Stocks

### Oil and Energy
- **PVD (Petrovietnam Drilling):** upstream drilling contractor; revenue and margins positively correlated with Brent crude price; break-even typically $40-50/barrel for rig utilization
- **PVS (Petrovietnam Technical Services):** oilfield services; offshore construction and inspection; benefits from high oil prices driving E&P investment
- **GAS (PetroVietnam Gas JSC):** gas distribution monopoly; revenues linked to oil-indexed LNG prices; margin relatively stable but benefits from volume growth
- **PLX (Petrolimex):** fuel retail; complex — benefits from stable fuel prices and volume, but margin sensitive to retail price controls vs. import cost

**Oil price impact rule:** Brent above $80 = net positive for Vietnam oil/gas sector stocks (PVD, PVS, GAS); but also raises import costs for fuel-dependent industries (transport, utilities) — mixed for overall market. Vietnam is a net importer of refined petroleum products, so sustained $100+ oil = mild macro negative for trade balance.

### Steel
- **HPG (Hoa Phat Group):** Vietnam's dominant steel producer; vertical integration from raw material to steel products
- **HPG cost structure:** ~60% coking coal (imported, primarily from Australia), ~20% iron ore (imported), ~20% energy/other
- **Coking coal price impact:** $50/tonne change in coking coal = significant HPG margin impact; monitor Australian coking coal prices as leading indicator
- **Chinese steel dumping risk:** China overcapacity → Chinese steel exports at below-cost prices → Vietnam steel prices compressed → HPG margins squeezed even if input costs fall
- **HPG demand drivers:** domestic construction (60% of steel use), export markets when domestic demand soft (primarily ASEAN)

### Agricultural Commodities
- **VNM (Vinamilk):** largest dairy company; input costs include corn and soybean meal (for cattle feed)
  - Corn/soy price spike → VNM input cost increase → margin pressure unless VNM can raise retail prices (limited power in consumer market)
  - VNM also imports raw milk powder from New Zealand — NZD price matters
- **DBC (Dabaco):** animal feed + pork production; highly sensitive to corn/soybean prices (feed inputs) and pork prices (revenue)
  - African Swine Fever outbreaks historically create severe volatility in pork/hog cycles — track ASF news carefully
- **VHC (Vinh Hoan):** pangasius (catfish) export; dollar revenues from US and EU; input costs include fish feed (corn/soy derived)
  - Fish meal prices as input; VHC margin = catfish export price − feed cost − processing
- **ANV (Nam Viet Corporation):** pangasius exporter; similar dynamics to VHC but smaller scale

### Electronics / Semiconductor Ecosystem
- Samsung's Vietnam operations are not publicly listed, but Samsung Vietnam's performance is a major input to:
  - Vietnam's export data (Samsung = ~20% of total exports)
  - Industrial zone demand (Samsung facilities are in KBC, Bac Ninh areas)
  - Supplier ecosystem (multiple tier-1 suppliers listed on HOSE/HNX)
- **How to track:** Samsung's global revenue guidance (released quarterly in South Korea); Samsung's CapEx announcement for Vietnam-specific expansion
- **Impact chain:** Samsung strong quarter → Vietnam electronics exports beat → VN-Index sentiment positive → industrial zone stocks (KBC, SIP) benefit
