# Emerging Market Framework for Vietnam

## 1. Vietnam's Current Market Classification

### The Classification Hierarchy
Global equity indices classify markets along a spectrum from Developed to Frontier, with Emerging Market in between. Classification determines which index funds hold Vietnam equities, and thus how much passive capital flows into the market.

**Current status (as of 2024):**
- **MSCI:** Frontier Market — below Emerging Market; Vietnam sits alongside Morocco, Kazakhstan, Nigeria
- **FTSE Russell:** Secondary Emerging Market (also called "Watch List for Primary Emerging") — one classification step below Primary Emerging Market, where the major EM funds invest

### Why Classification Matters for Investment
The capital flows differential between classification tiers is enormous:

| Classification | Estimated Global AUM Benchmarked | Vietnam Passive Inflow Estimate |
|---------------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| MSCI Frontier | ~$20-30B | Vietnam already included; ~$200-400M passive |
| MSCI Emerging Market | ~$2.5 trillion | Vietnam inclusion: estimated $1-3B new passive demand |
| FTSE Frontier | ~$10-15B | Vietnam included; modest passive |
| FTSE Primary Emerging | ~$800B | Vietnam inclusion: estimated $1.5-2B new passive demand |

**Investment thesis:** Upgrading from Frontier/Secondary EM to Primary EM is a major re-rating catalyst. Stocks that get included in EM indices see immediate demand from passive funds (ETFs, index funds) that must buy them by weight — regardless of price. This creates a one-time repricing event followed by sustained higher liquidity.

### Stocks Most Likely to Be Included in EM Indices
MSCI and FTSE have size and liquidity screens. Vietnam's largest and most liquid stocks are:
1. **VCB (Vietcombank)** — largest cap; most liquid; international accounting standards; strong candidate
2. **TCB (Techcombank)** — large private bank; well-followed by foreigners; strong candidate
3. **VHM (Vinhomes)** — largest real estate company; already at FOL in foreign ownership; candidate but FOL is a problem
4. **HPG (Hoa Phat Group)** — largest industrial; well-known among EM investors; candidate
5. **MSN (Masan Group)** — consumer conglomerate; complex structure but large cap; candidate
6. **VNM (Vinamilk)** — most internationally familiar Vietnam brand; already partially foreign-owned; candidate
7. **FPT (FPT Corporation)** — tech company; clean accounts; internationally recognized; candidate
8. **MWG (Mobile World)** — largest electronics retailer; domestic-focused but large cap; candidate if size/liquidity met

## 2. MSCI EM Upgrade Criteria — What Vietnam Is Missing

### MSCI Classification Requirements
MSCI uses a three-criteria framework: Economic Development, Size and Liquidity, and Market Accessibility. Vietnam passes economic development (upper-middle income threshold) and has stocks meeting size/liquidity (VN30 top names). **Market Accessibility is the barrier.**

### Barrier 1: DvP (Delivery vs. Payment) Settlement
**What MSCI requires:** Settlement must occur on a Delivery vs. Payment basis — meaning securities and cash change hands simultaneously. This eliminates counterparty risk.

**Vietnam's current system:** Vietnam requires pre-funding — buyers must deposit 100% of the purchase amount before trading begins. This means:
- Foreign investors must wire money to Vietnam BEFORE they know what price they'll pay
- This is operationally burdensome for large institutional investors
- It prevents same-day allocation of capital to multiple markets simultaneously

**The solution being developed:** Non-Prefunded Trading (NTP) — allows qualified foreign institutional investors to trade without pre-depositing funds, with settlement guaranteed by custodian banks. The SSC (State Securities Commission) and HOSE have been developing NTP framework since 2022-2023.

**Timeline:** NTP implementation depends on regulatory approval and custodian bank infrastructure readiness. Market observers estimate 2025-2026 as realistic timeline for operational NTP that satisfies MSCI DvP criteria.

### Barrier 2: Foreign Ownership Limits (FOL) and Market Accessibility
MSCI requires that foreign investors can actually buy the securities they need to track the index:
- **Foreign Ownership Limits:** Many Vietnam stocks at 49% FOL (for non-banking stocks) or 30% FOL (for banking stocks); when stocks are at FOL, foreign investors cannot buy → MSCI cannot include them in the index as accessible
- **Solution options:** (1) Increase or remove FOL for sectors where Vietnam is willing to allow higher foreign participation, (2) Create depository receipt structures that track underlying shares, (3) Accept bifurcated treatment where stocks at FOL are excluded from index

### Barrier 3: Vietnamese Market Size and Liquidity (Largely Met)
MSCI requires a minimum number of securities meeting size/liquidity thresholds. Vietnam currently meets this for approximately 3-5 of the VN30 blue chips. This is borderline — close enough that other barriers dominate the decision.

### MSCI Watch List Status
Vietnam has been on MSCI's "watch list for potential reclassification to Emerging Markets" for multiple cycles. Being on the watch list means:
- MSCI is actively evaluating Vietnam
- Annual feedback reports are published
- Upgrade could happen in any given year if criteria are met
- Market participants price in a "probability" — estimated 20-30% per year once major barriers are removed

## 3. FTSE Upgrade Criteria and Timeline

### FTSE's Approach vs. MSCI
FTSE Russell has slightly different and arguably more achievable criteria for Vietnam:
- FTSE already classifies Vietnam as **Secondary Emerging Market** (not Frontier like MSCI) — one step closer to Primary EM
- FTSE's criteria are more quantitative (size/liquidity focused) and less focused on settlement mechanism specifics
- FTSE has had Vietnam on its watch list since **2018** — longest-running watch list case globally

### Current FTSE Status
- Vietnam is on FTSE watch list for upgrade from Secondary to Primary Emerging Market
- FTSE has flagged the same issues as MSCI: DvP settlement, FOL caps, pre-registration for foreign investors
- FTSE has stated that progress on NTP could be sufficient to trigger an upgrade decision

### Estimated Passive Inflow on FTSE Primary EM Upgrade
- FTSE Emerging Markets index funds (e.g., Vanguard Emerging Markets ETF VWOB, iShares MSCI Emerging Markets) benchmark ~$800B globally
- Vietnam weight on inclusion: estimated 0.25-0.35% based on market cap
- Passive inflow: $800B × 0.30% = **~$2.4B** (rough estimate, front-end loaded as ETFs rebalance)
- This inflow would be concentrated in the top 7-10 eligible Vietnam stocks over approximately 1-3 months post-announcement

### Announcement vs. Implementation Timeline
**Critical distinction:** MSCI and FTSE typically announce upgrades 12-18 months before implementation.
- Announcement = upgrade is confirmed but implementation date is set in the future
- The announcement itself triggers price action as active investors position ahead of passive forced buying
- Implementation date = when passive funds actually buy
- The trade: position in EM upgrade candidates BEFORE the announcement; the announcement-to-implementation period sees continued buying from both active (front-running) and passive (forced rebalancing at implementation)

## 4. Investment Implications of Frontier vs. EM Status

### Frontier Market Investor Base (Current State)
Vietnam's current investor base in Frontier status:
- **Frontier-specialized funds:** Small, specialized funds (Dragon Capital, VinaCapital, PXP Vietnam) — combined AUM ~$3-5B; these are already invested
- **Global EM funds with Frontier allocation:** Some EM funds have a small Frontier sleeve (~5-10% of AUM) — limited additional buying from this segment
- **Direct foreign investors:** Individual and institutional investors who specifically target Vietnam without index mandate — opportunistic, not systematic

**Implication:** Frontier market investment is dominated by specialists; price discovery is inefficient; liquidity thin; small capital flows can move markets significantly.

### Primary EM Investor Base (Target State)
Upon EM upgrade:
- **Passive EM index funds:** Must buy Vietnam at index weight; buying is mechanical and price-insensitive
- **Active EM funds:** Must benchmark against EM index; if underweight Vietnam vs. index = active underweight risk → pressure to buy up to weight
- **Global asset allocators:** Vietnam becomes part of standard EM allocation for pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, endowments
- **Total incremental capital:** $3-5B estimated over 12-24 months post-effective upgrade date

### Valuation Impact
Stocks included in EM indices trade at meaningful premium to equivalent Frontier stocks because:
1. More buyers (higher demand) vs. the same supply of shares
2. Better price discovery (more research coverage, more liquidity)
3. Institutional quality signal (EM inclusion = meets global standards)
4. Lower risk premium (more liquid = lower liquidity discount)

**Historical examples:**
- Pakistan's MSCI EM inclusion (2017): KSE-100 Index +40% in 12 months preceding inclusion date
- Saudi Arabia's MSCI EM inclusion (2019): Saudi stocks re-rated; P/E multiple expanded 2-3× in upgrade cycle
- China A-shares partial EM inclusion: massive passive flows, volatility in short-term but sustained higher liquidity

### Clarke's Approach to EM Upgrade Theme
Vietnam's EM upgrade is NOT a base case — it is an option:
- Base case: Vietnam remains Frontier/Secondary EM; organic growth drives returns
- Upgrade scenario: NTP implemented + FTSE/MSCI criteria met → re-rating catalyst of 20-40% for index in aggregate

**Portfolio construction rule:** EM upgrade candidates (VCB, TCB, VHM, HPG, MSN, FPT) already merit investment on fundamental grounds. The EM upgrade thesis adds upside optionality — it is not a reason to pay an excessive premium today, but it means the downside is cushioned by fundamental value AND the upside is enhanced by potential re-rating.
