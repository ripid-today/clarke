# Vietnam Macro Framework

## 1. Vietnam's Macroeconomic Structure

### GDP Composition
Vietnam's GDP structure as of 2024 (~$430B nominal):
- **Private consumption:** ~68% of GDP — dominant driver; urban middle-class expansion is the structural growth story
- **Investment (FDI + domestic):** ~25% of GDP — FDI consistently $18-22B annually in recent years; domestic investment driven by state capital and private sector credit expansion
- **Government spending:** ~7% of GDP — public investment acceleration has been a policy lever since 2022 when private sector slowed
- **Net exports:** small positive contributor; Vietnam has been a consistent goods trade surplus country since ~2012

### 2024 GDP Benchmarks
- Nominal GDP: ~$430B (World Bank 2024 estimate)
- GDP per capita: ~$4,300 (approaching upper-middle income threshold of $4,500)
- Growth target 2025: 6.5-7.0% (government/National Assembly target); World Bank and IMF consensus ~6.0-6.5%
- Growth drivers ranked: (1) manufacturing exports, (2) domestic consumption recovery, (3) public investment acceleration
- Long-run nominal GDP growth: ~9-10% (real 6-7% + inflation 3-4%)

### FDI Structure — The Manufacturing Engine
FDI is Vietnam's primary growth accelerant and the mechanism linking Vietnam to global supply chains.

Key anchor investors (as of 2024):
- **Samsung** — largest single FDI investor; semiconductor/display manufacturing in Bac Ninh, Thai Nguyen, Ho Chi Minh City; revenues are dollar-denominated and export-oriented; Samsung alone accounts for ~20% of Vietnam's total exports
- **Intel Products Vietnam** — chip assembly and testing in HCMC (Saigon Hi-Tech Park); significant employer of skilled technicians
- **Nike / Adidas** — footwear manufacturing in HCMC and surrounding provinces; major employer in Binh Duong, Dong Nai; labor-cost-driven; dollar revenues
- **LEGO** — $1B factory in Binh Duong (announced 2022, operational phases 2024-2026); represents supply chain diversification away from China
- **LG Electronics** — display components in Haiphong
- Broader ecosystem: hundreds of tier-1 and tier-2 suppliers to Samsung and other anchors have followed — electrical components, plastic parts, PCB manufacturing

**Investment implication:** FDI confidence is the leading indicator for Vietnam's medium-term growth story. Track Samsung global revenue guidance and CapEx announcements as proxy for Vietnam FDI outlook. Any slowdown in electronics sector globally hits Vietnam export data within 2-3 months.

### Domestic Consumption
- Urban middle class: ~28M people (2024) projected to reach 44M by 2030 — key demand driver for consumer staples, finance, retail
- Wage growth: urban formal sector wages growing ~8-10% nominal annually; rural wages slower at ~5-7%
- Consumer credit: growing rapidly — banks have expanded personal loan books; credit card penetration still low vs. regional peers = structural growth runway
- E-commerce: Shopee and TikTok Shop dominant; logistics investment accelerating; VHM and REITs benefiting from warehouse demand

## 2. Inflation and Monetary Policy

### CPI Structure and Targets
- **Full-year CPI target:** 4.5% (joint SBV/government target, set annually by National Assembly)
- CPI basket composition: food & beverages ~35-36% (of which pork is a meaningful sub-component), housing & utilities ~16%, transport ~9%, healthcare ~6%, education ~6%, other ~28%
- **Food prices:** historically the most volatile CPI component; pork price swings of 20-30% have historically added/subtracted 0.5-1.0 percentage points from headline CPI
- **Weather-driven agriculture component:** typhoon or drought affecting rice production (Mekong Delta) creates food price spikes; flooding impacts vegetable prices

### SBV Monetary Policy Tools
Three primary transmission mechanisms:

1. **OMO (Open Market Operations):** SBV's most frequently used tool for short-term liquidity management
   - SBV injects liquidity → overnight interbank rate falls → commercial bank funding cost drops → lending rates ease within weeks
   - SBV drains liquidity → interbank rates rise → credit tightens → 2-3 month lag to lending rate impact
   - How to read: SBV net OMO position (available on sbv.gov.vn) — net injection = accommodative; net drain = tightening signal

2. **Refinancing rate (policy rate):** headline signal tool; sets the floor for interbank lending rates
   - 2023 policy history: SBV cut aggressively — from 6.0% to 4.5% refinancing rate across 4 cuts in 2023
   - Current level (2024): 4.5% — historically low; SBV signaling support for growth
   - Transmission lag: refinancing rate cut → commercial bank prime rate adjustment: 2-3 months lag
   - Investment signal: refinancing rate cut = bullish for credit-sensitive sectors (banks, real estate, consumer finance, rate-sensitive industrial)

3. **Required reserve ratio (RRR):** structural liquidity tool; used infrequently
   - Standard requirement: 3% for VND deposits; 8% for USD deposits (discouraging dollarization)
   - RRR cut = significant liquidity injection = strong stimulus signal; not used in normal cycles

### Policy Rate History and Context
| Date | Refinancing Rate | Context |
|------|-----------------|---------|
| End 2022 | 6.0% | Post-COVID tightening; inflation concern |
| Mar 2023 | 5.5% | Start of easing cycle |
| Jun 2023 | 4.5% | Aggressive support for slowing growth |
| 2024 | 4.5% (held) | Maintaining accommodative stance |

**Key interpretation rule:** SBV tends to prioritize growth over inflation control except when CPI threatens to breach 4.5% target AND VND faces depreciation pressure simultaneously. If CPI is below target and VND is stable, SBV is free to ease.

### Reading SBV Actions for Investment
- **Refinancing rate cut:** Bullish for all credit-sensitive sectors; banks see initial NIM compression but subsequent credit growth acceleration more than compensates (2-quarter lag rule)
- **OMO net injection (multi-week):** Bullish for banking liquidity; short-term interbank rates fall; watch for lending rate transmission over 4-8 weeks
- **VND defense (SBV sells USD):** Tightening signal; absorbs VND liquidity from system; bearish for equities short-term

## 3. Currency (VND) Framework

### Exchange Rate Regime
- **Managed float with ±5% band** around SBV's daily reference rate
- SBV publishes reference rate each morning before market open; ±5% defines intraday trading range for commercial banks
- Not a free float — SBV has significant discretion to adjust the reference rate trajectory

### Reserve Adequacy
- Foreign exchange reserves: ~$88-95B (2024 estimate; fluctuates with intervention)
- Import coverage: ~3.2-3.8 months of imports (IMF adequate threshold: 3 months)
- Reserve drawdown in 2022: SBV spent ~$20B defending VND during USD strength; partially rebuilt in 2023
- Significance: reserves below 3 months = speculative pressure on VND possible; current level is adequate but not comfortable

### Remittance Flows — An Underappreciated Buffer
- Annual remittances: ~$16-18B per year (2023-24 data from World Bank/SBV)
- Source: ~5.5M Vietnamese diaspora, primarily in US, EU, Australia, Japan, Korea
- Remittances are relatively stable vs. FDI and portfolio flows — structural support for VND
- During USD strength, remittance senders often time transfers to maximize VND receipts = natural VND demand

### VND Depreciation Risk and Triggers
Vietnam has experienced three meaningful VND depreciation episodes in the past decade:

1. **2015:** USD appreciation + China yuan devaluation → VND pressure → SBV allowed stepwise devaluation
2. **2018:** Fed tightening → EM pressure → VND −2% vs USD
3. **2022:** Most significant — Fed's fastest rate-hiking cycle in 40 years → USD Index spiked → VND −4.5% officially, but black market wider

**Depreciation triggers (watch for all three simultaneously):**
- Trade deficit widens significantly (imports > exports + remittances + FDI)
- Capital outflows accelerate (foreign investors selling Vietnam equities + bonds)
- USD Index (DXY) above 105 and rising

### Currency Impact on Vietnam Investments
| Currency Movement | Winners | Losers |
|------------------|---------|--------|
| VND weakens vs USD | Exporters (textile, seafood, electronics ecosystem), FDI-linked companies | Importers (fuel, raw materials, consumer goods importers) |
| VND strengthens vs USD | Importers, domestic consumer plays | Exporters (margin compression) |

**Specific examples:**
- **VND weakness winners:** VHC (seafood — dollar revenues, VND costs), ANV (seafood), TCM (textiles — dollar export revenues), FPT (offshore IT — dollar revenues)
- **VND weakness losers:** PLC (imports petroleum products priced in USD), input-dependent manufacturers with no pricing power
- **Neutral:** HPG (Hoa Phat) — both exports and imports in USD; partially hedged naturally

## 4. Key Data Calendar and Sources

### Monthly Data Releases (GSO)
| Indicator | Release Timing | What to Watch |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| CPI | First week of following month | MoM and YoY; food sub-index especially |
| IIP (Industrial Production Index) | First week | Manufacturing output growth signal |
| Retail sales | First week | Domestic consumption health |
| Trade balance | Mid-month (preliminary) | Export growth (esp. electronics) vs. imports |
| FDI disbursement | End of month | Registered + disbursed; realized FDI is the real signal |

### Monthly Data Releases (SBV)
| Indicator | Release Timing | What to Watch |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Credit growth (year-to-date) | Monthly | YTD credit growth vs. annual target (~14-15%) |
| M2 money supply | Monthly | Broad liquidity measure; M2 growth drives asset prices with 6-12 month lag |
| Exchange rate | Daily | SBV reference rate published each morning |

### Quarterly Releases
- **GDP (GSO):** Released approximately 4 weeks after quarter end; preliminary, then revised
- **Current account balance (SBV):** Quarterly; shows FDI, remittance, trade in full picture

### Annual Releases
- **Public debt / budget (MoF):** Annual; public debt/GDP below 60% is legal ceiling
- **State Budget execution (MoF):** Shows actual capital disbursement vs. plan — key for infrastructure investment theme

## 5. How Macro Data Flows to Investment Thesis

### Transmission Chain 1: SBV Rate Cuts → Banks
1. SBV cuts refinancing rate
2. Interbank overnight rate falls (immediate, 1-2 weeks)
3. Commercial bank deposit rates cut (4-6 weeks)
4. Commercial bank lending rates cut (6-8 weeks)
5. Credit demand picks up as loans become cheaper (2-3 months)
6. **Bank NIM compresses initially (negative for 1-2 quarters), then credit growth acceleration drives higher net interest income** — net positive for bank sector after 2-quarter lag
7. **Investment signal:** buy bank stocks 1-2 quarters before credit growth acceleration becomes visible in data

### Transmission Chain 2: VND Weakness → Exporters
1. DXY rises OR trade deficit widens OR capital outflows accelerate
2. VND depreciates vs. USD
3. Exporters' VND-equivalent revenues rise (they sell in USD, costs are in VND)
4. Exporter gross margins expand
5. **Profit surprises in next quarterly report**
6. **Investment signal:** VND weakness = rotate toward exporters (seafood, textiles, FPT for IT services)

### Transmission Chain 3: Public Investment Acceleration → Construction/Materials
1. Government sets ambitious CapEx target (e.g., road, metro, airport expansion)
2. MoF releases capital allocation to ministries and provinces
3. Actual disbursement flows to contractors and materials suppliers (usually with 3-6 month lag vs. announcement)
4. Demand for steel, cement, construction equipment rises
5. **Investment signal:** watch actual disbursement data (MoF monthly), not just announcements; HPG (steel), VCS (electrical infrastructure), CTD (construction), PHC (civil engineering) benefit

### Transmission Chain 4: Credit Growth Acceleration → Rate-Sensitive Sectors
1. SBV sets annual credit growth target (typically 14-15% in recent years)
2. As credit quota utilization rises through year, bank lending accelerates
3. More credit available for real estate mortgages → real estate demand picks up
4. Real estate prices stabilize → developer pre-sales improve → cash flow into sector
5. **Investment signal:** credit growth above 12% YTD by July = bullish for real estate stocks; below 8% = continued stress

### Transmission Chain 5: FDI Confidence → Industrial REITs and Supporting Infrastructure
1. Samsung / Intel / LEGO announce expansion plans
2. Industrial zone operators see land demand pick up
3. Industrial zone REITs (SIP, KBC, VGC) benefit via higher land lease prices and new development
4. Supporting infrastructure (logistics, utilities) benefits
5. **Investment signal:** track FDI registration data and specific anchor investor announcements; 3-6 month lead time to financial impact
