# Price Action and Chart Patterns

## 1. Candlestick Reversal Patterns

Candlestick patterns are observations of short-term price psychology — who was in control during a session and whether that control is shifting. They are signals, not certainties; always require confirmation from volume and broader context.

### Single-Candle Reversal Signals

**Doji**
- **Appearance:** Open price ≈ Close price; small or absent body; shadows on both sides
- **Meaning:** Indecision — neither bulls nor bears won the session; the market is pausing
- **Conditions for actionability:** Only meaningful near significant support or resistance levels; a doji in the middle of a range tells you nothing useful
- **Confirmation required:** Next session close in the direction of the expected reversal; volume on confirmation day > average
- **Vietnam note:** Dojis on ceiling (trần) or floor (sàn) days are regulatory artifacts, not genuine indecision — exclude from analysis

**Hammer (Bullish Reversal at Bottom)**
- **Appearance:** Small real body (open and close near top of session range); long lower shadow at least 2× the body length; little or no upper shadow
- **Meaning:** Sellers pushed price lower during the session, but buyers absorbed the selling and pushed price back up to near the open — potential bottom
- **Conditions for actionability:** Must appear after a downtrend (meaningless as a reversal signal in an uptrend); must be near known support
- **Confirmation:** Next 3-5 sessions hold above the hammer's low; volume on the hammer day ideally above average (buyers active)
- **False signal risk:** Hammer at support fails if support level itself fails; always set stop loss below the hammer's low

**Shooting Star (Bearish Reversal at Top)**
- **Appearance:** Small real body near bottom of session range; long upper shadow at least 2× body; little or no lower shadow — inverted hammer shape
- **Meaning:** Buyers tried to push higher but sellers overwhelmed them and pushed price back down; potential exhaustion at top
- **Conditions for actionability:** Must appear after an uptrend; must be near known resistance
- **Confirmation:** Next session closes below the shooting star's body; increased volume on the downside confirmation day

**Inverted Hammer (Potential Bullish at Bottom)**
- Same visual as shooting star but appearing at the bottom of a downtrend
- Less reliable than hammer; requires strong next-session confirmation
- Interpretation: buyers attempted to recover but didn't fully succeed yet; watch next session closely

### Multi-Candle Reversal Patterns

**Bullish Engulfing (Bottom Reversal)**
- **Appearance:** Two-candle pattern; first candle is bearish (red/down); second candle is bullish (green/up) with body that completely engulfs (covers) the prior day's body
- **Meaning:** Bears were in control, then bulls completely overpowered them on the following session
- **Volume confirmation:** Volume on the engulfing (second) candle must be higher than the first; ideally 1.5-2× average
- **Strength hierarchy:** The more the second candle engulfs (covers shadows too), the stronger the signal
- **Vietnam application:** Bullish engulfing after 3-5 session downtrend near key support = high conviction entry signal

**Bearish Engulfing (Top Reversal)**
- Opposite of bullish engulfing; appears at top of uptrend
- First candle bullish; second candle bearish body engulfs the first
- Volume confirmation same requirement
- Often the first warning sign of trend reversal for trending stocks

**Morning Star (Three-Candle Bottom Reversal)**
- **Day 1:** Large bearish candle (sellers in control; significant down move)
- **Day 2:** Small-bodied candle (doji or small body) — indecision; sellers losing conviction
- **Day 3:** Large bullish candle closing well into the body of Day 1's candle
- **Volume pattern:** Day 1 high volume (capitulation); Day 2 low volume; Day 3 high volume (buyers returning)
- **Reliability:** One of the most reliable three-candle reversal patterns; requires all three elements to be valid
- **Target entry:** Buy on close of Day 3 or open of Day 4; stop below Day 1's low

**Evening Star (Three-Candle Top Reversal)**
- Exact mirror of Morning Star at an uptrend top
- Day 1: large bullish; Day 2: small doji; Day 3: large bearish
- Often precedes significant downtrend; exit or short signal

**Three White Soldiers (Continuation Uptrend Confirmation)**
- Three consecutive bullish candles, each opening inside the prior body and closing near the high
- Signals strong buying momentum; confirms breakout or recovery
- **Volume rule:** Each successive candle should have equal or higher volume

**Three Black Crows (Continuation Downtrend Confirmation)**
- Three consecutive bearish candles, each opening inside the prior body and closing near the low
- Signals strong selling pressure; confirms breakdown

## 2. Chart Patterns with Measured Move Targets

Chart patterns represent multi-session price psychology playing out at scale. The measured move calculation gives a price target; it is probabilistic, not guaranteed.

### Reversal Patterns

**Head and Shoulders (Top Reversal)**
- **Structure:** Left shoulder (rally + pullback), Head (higher rally + deeper pullback), Right shoulder (lower rally than head + pullback to neckline)
- **Neckline:** Line connecting the two lows between the shoulders; this is the critical level
- **Confirmation:** Price breaks below neckline with volume surge
- **Measured move target:** Take the height from neckline to top of head → project downward from neckline breakout point
  - Example: Neckline at VND 30,000; Head at VND 38,000; Height = VND 8,000; Target = VND 30,000 − VND 8,000 = VND 22,000
- **Entry strategy:** Enter short (or exit long) on confirmed neckline break with volume; stop above right shoulder high
- **Vietnam note:** Use daily chart; ignore intraday noise; pattern must be visible on weekly chart too for high-conviction calls

**Inverse Head and Shoulders (Bottom Reversal)**
- Mirror image of H&S; left shoulder, head (lower), right shoulder; neckline connects the highs between shoulders
- Confirmation: price breaks above neckline with volume surge
- Measured move target: height from neckline to head bottom, projected upward from breakout
- One of the most reliable bottom reversal patterns in technical analysis literature

**Double Top (Top Reversal)**
- Two peaks at approximately the same price level; neckline is the low between the two peaks
- Confirmation: price closes below neckline
- Measured move: distance from peaks to neckline, projected down from neckline break
- Second peak often forms on lower volume than the first (momentum divergence) — look for this confirmation
- **Vietnam example:** Many Vietnam stocks have formed double tops near historical highs before major corrections

**Double Bottom (Bottom Reversal)**
- Two troughs at approximately the same price; neckline is the high between the two troughs
- Confirmation: price closes above neckline with volume surge
- Measured move: distance from neckline to trough bottom, projected up from neckline break
- The "W" pattern; one of the most commonly observed bottom formations in liquid stocks

### Continuation Patterns

**Cup and Handle (Bullish Continuation)**
- **Cup:** 1-3 month rounded base; price declines gradually, rounds the bottom, and rises back to the starting level (like a cup shape when viewed on a chart)
- **Handle:** Smaller consolidation (flag or triangle) after the cup; 1-3 weeks; price dips slightly from cup high
- **Breakout:** Price breaks above the handle's resistance with volume surge
- **Measured move target:** Depth of cup + breakout point
  - Example: Stock at VND 50,000 at start, drops to VND 38,000 (cup depth = VND 12,000), recovers to VND 50,000, forms handle; breakout at VND 51,000; target = VND 51,000 + VND 12,000 = VND 63,000
- **Vietnam application:** Commonly appears on longer-term weekly charts for blue chips recovering from sector corrections

**Bull Flag (Continuation after Strong Move)**
- **Pole:** Sharp, powerful upward move (5-15% in a few sessions)
- **Flag:** Tight, slightly downward-sloping consolidation over 1-2 weeks; low volume during consolidation
- **Breakout:** Price breaks above the flag's upper boundary with volume expansion
- **Measured move:** Pole height + flag breakout point
- High reward-to-risk: tight stop just below the flag; target is full pole height replicated

**Bear Flag (Continuation after Strong Down Move)**
- Mirror image of bull flag; downward pole + slightly upward-sloping consolidation + downward breakout
- Lower volume during consolidation, volume expansion on breakdown confirms

**Ascending Triangle (Bullish)**
- Flat resistance (multiple tests at same level) + rising support trendline (higher lows)
- Compression before breakout; breakout typically resolves to the upside
- Measured move: height of triangle (widest point) added to breakout level

**Descending Triangle (Bearish)**
- Flat support (multiple tests at same level) + falling resistance trendline (lower highs)
- Typically resolves to the downside
- Measured move: height of triangle subtracted from breakdown level

## 3. Support and Resistance Analysis

### Identifying Support and Resistance Levels

**Prior Highs and Lows**
- The most reliable and objective form of S/R; price has memory
- The more times a level has been tested and held, the stronger it becomes
- **Prior resistance becomes support after breakout:** If a stock breaks above VND 30,000 and holds, that VND 30,000 level becomes support on the next test
- **Prior support becomes resistance after breakdown:** The reverse; classic "role reversal"
- **Number of tests:** 1 test = weak; 2 tests = moderate; 3+ tests = strong; 4+ tests = very strong (and the eventual break will be powerful when it comes)

**Round Number Psychology**
- Vietnam-specific: market participants strongly anchor to round VND numbers
- Key psychological levels: 10,000; 20,000; 30,000; 40,000; 50,000; 100,000 VND
- Stocks cluster around these levels; first test of a round number = resistance; breaking through = next round number becomes the target
- **Practical application:** When calculating price targets via measured move, note if the target falls near a round number — cluster targets to round numbers (the move often stalls there first)

**Volume Profile Support and Resistance**
- High-volume price zones = strong S/R; many positions were opened there and owners defend their entry
- Low-volume "air pockets" above or below congestion zones = price moves quickly through these zones
- **Identifying high-volume zones:** Look at volume bars on chart; note price levels where volume spikes; those levels are S/R

**Moving Averages as Dynamic S/R**
- SMA20 in strong uptrend: first pullback target; often holds in trending stocks
- SMA50: most widely-watched medium-term S/R; institutional reference level
- SMA200: structural S/R; stocks rally from SMA200 in bull markets; fail at SMA200 in bear markets
- **How to use:** Price testing SMA50 from above in uptrend = potential entry; price failing to get above SMA200 in downtrend = resistance confirmation
